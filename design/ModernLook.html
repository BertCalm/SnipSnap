What stays, what moves

A TapeOS scheme is a token swap and nothing else. The OpenSky page needs that and a second thing the current system has no slot for: a different way of drawing the same tokens. So the proposal is two axes, not one longer list of schemes.

Where the audition page already agrees with TapeOS

  • Two surfaces. The page's hero is paper and its player dock is near-black with orange for the live light. That is the two-surface rule in a different coat, and it is why the LCD can stay dark without the modern look feeling bolted on.
  • Scanlines. The page draws the sun with a 2px-on, 1px-off stripe at low opacity. TapeOS draws its LCD with a 1px line every 3px. The modern LCD keeps the stripe, at the sun's opacity rather than the LCD's.
  • Handwriting becomes italic. TapeOS sets everything a person named in a marker face. The page sets its titles in serif italic. In the modern look, a user's own names (kits, pads, cassettes) are the only italic text on screen, so the rule survives with a different face.
  • Mono for the machine. The page uses a monospace face for eyebrows, IDs and meta. TapeOS uses Silkscreen for pad IDs, status and hints. Same job, same slot.

Open questions

  1. Is OPENSKY one scheme in a new look, or the first of a family? The page gives one palette. More colourways (a dusk one, a dark-paper one) are a design job, not a code one.
  2. Does the modern look get its own type, or only its own surfaces? Type is the bigger change in code and the bigger change on screen.
  3. CLEAR, the light scheme, was cut in August because light shells "didn't land". This look is light. Is the difference (a flat, printed look rather than a pale bevelled one) enough to reopen that?

The mockup draws the KIT screen only. PLAY (all LCD, no chrome) would look nearly the same in both looks, which is the point of the rule.
