What is being tested

A duet inside the sound.

Each harp has four independently plucked strings, its own body response, and four sympathetic resonators. The upper bank begins one octave above the bass bank. The revision gives their plucks a richer harmonic spectrum and more distinct body colors. Longer ringing lets you follow the sound after the strum has finished.

A shared current changes the strings’ decay and the transfer into unplucked resonators, while moving the venue’s transmission paths. The still/moving pair holds the strum steady; the venue-bypassed probes let you judge the instrument-side response.

Coral uses short reflections and small cavities. Seaweed uses soft, changing paths without an enclosed late reverberation tank.

Listen for
  • Does soft touch have a different color from bright touch?
  • Does the ringing develop after the last pluck?
  • Can you hear still water become moving water?
  • Does sympathetic bloom add a distinct answer?
  • Do coral and seaweed sound like different places?
Model 2 retains one voice, PEARL, with coral and seaweed venues. The original model is preserved for recipe replay and the opening A/B comparison. Conch, cave, held loops, expanded voicings, and live synthesis controls remain later work. Listening acceptance is pending.
Reproducible listening

Audio, recipes, and evidence.

The opening full-mix pairs are individually matched for level, with shared peak headroom within each pair. Mechanism pairs, control sweeps, and component stems share a reference gain to preserve their natural balance. Common raw gain applies one safe gain across the whole pack.

Solo stem and bypass clips are diagnostic probes. Their probe settings are saved beside the production recipe in the manifest and diagnostics files. No external effect chain is used.
