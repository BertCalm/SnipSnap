# The MUTATE Card's Round M1 (Words and Honesty) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** The MUTATE card says what each control will do before it is tapped, and every button tells the truth. Each move gets its line from the house's copy board, and the knob gets its meaning and sits under the moves. The dead `—` rows become words, and PARTNER becomes the one word for the second sound. HEAR, KEEP and UNDO are dimmed, not disabled, and say why. The commit verb becomes KEEP, with `DRIFT · BLEND & SAVE` beside it, and the result plays after KEEP and DRIFT. Refusals are typed (layered, chained, no partner, empty shelf, only doubles, partner gone). The keep toast reports a STACK flip and a length change, and UNDO's toast says what actually came back. This is round M1 of three; it ends with the owner's phone check, which also closes BECOME's pending A1b check (QQ4).

**Architecture:** `:app` cannot be compiled here, so every decision the card makes moves into `:shell` first, as a pure function with JUnit tests written first and watched failing:
- the card's words in `MutateSheet` (`outcomeLine`, `knobMeaning`, `deadKnobLine`, `becomeMeaning`, `partnerName`, `partnerShort`, `pairLine`, `pairSpoken`, the labels), held to the house style by a new `MutateWordsTest`;
- the typed refusals (`Refusal`, `refusalBefore`, `refusalOf`, `undoRefusal`, `PartnerGone`, `Mutate.RouletteRefused`);
- the toasts in `Copy` (PARTNER's words, the refusal lines, `mutated` with the flip and the length, `lengthNote`);
- the length seam (`MutateSheet.lengthMs`).

Then `PadSheetScreen.kt` is edited in three steps, each opened by `ConventionTest` source-text laws run red first: the doors (Task 5), the card's words and buttons (Task 6), and the knob rows (Task 7, which first stops for the owner's answer to Decision 16). ## Decision 16, measured: the bar's width

The spec's default puts the knob's meaning after the value, on the knob's own line. It estimated the bar at "about 86 dp on a 390 dp sheet" and asks this plan to measure it "on the 390 dp board first". Here is the measurement, from the code at `3f155708` and the font file:

- **The card's width on a 390 dp phone is 334 dp.** Start from 390. Take off 2 × 12 dp for the frame's outer padding (`Layout.OUTER_MARGIN = 12`, `shell/src/main/kotlin/com/snipsnap/shell/Schemes.kt:343`, applied at `app/src/main/kotlin/com/snipsnap/app/App.kt:2722`). Take off 2 × 6 dp for the window frame's padding (`App.kt:2728`) and 2 × 10 dp for the open box's content padding (`app/src/main/kotlin/com/snipsnap/app/ui/GroupBox.kt:105`). The pad sheet's scroll column adds none (`PSS:2321`). The spec's estimate started from the full 390.
- **Today's bar is 218 dp.** `StepperSlider`'s row (`PSS:3115-3181`) is a 44 dp label column, an 8 dp gap, the bar (`weight(1f)`), another 8 dp gap and the 56 dp value column: 334 − 44 − 8 − 8 − 56 = 218 dp.
- **The meanings are 91.5 to 146.5 dp wide.** They are drawn in `TapeType.pixelSmall`: Silkscreen at 8 sp with 0.5 sp letter spacing (`app/src/main/kotlin/com/snipsnap/app/theme/TapeTheme.kt:87`). Widths are summed from the advance widths in `app/src/main/res/font/silkscreen.ttf` (1000 units per em), at a font scale of 1:
  - `· WHERE LOWS BECOME HIGHS` and `· HOW LONG THE TURN TAKES`: 146.5 dp;
  - `· HOW FINELY TONE IS READ`: 141.5 dp;
  - `· WHERE THEY HAND OVER`: 131.0 dp;
  - `· HOW FAR TOWARD THEM`: 124.5 dp;
  - `· HOW MUCH ROOM`: 91.5 dp.
- **With the meaning after the value, the bar is 64 to 119 dp.** One more 8 dp gap and the widest meaning leave 218 − 8 − 146.5 ≈ **64 dp** (HZ, and BECOME on MORPH). MORPH's MIX leaves about 85 dp and ROOM's WET about 118 dp.
  - At the 1/40 snap that is about 1.6 dp a step, against about 5.5 dp today. 64 dp is about 10 mm at the 160 dpi baseline, about a thumb's width.
  - On a 412 dp phone the widest leaves about 86 dp, the spec's figure.
  - A larger system font scales the meaning (it is in `sp`) and narrows the bar further.
- **Everything else fits** (same font, same method):
  - The dead rows: `NO KNOB — THEY LINE UP ON THE HIT` is 192.5 dp and `ONLY MORPH TURNS OVER TIME` 159.0 dp, inside the 282 dp right of the label column.
  - The note line: 249.0 dp.
  - The widest move line, `STARTS AS MINE, TURNS INTO THE MIX.`: 200.5 dp.
  - The widest 44-character pairing line, every glyph a W: 310.0 dp.
  - `ROULETTE · PICK A PARTNER OFF THE SHELF` at `ActionButton`'s 9 sp: 245.6 dp plus 16 dp padding.
  - `DRIFT · BLEND & SAVE` at 9 sp: 125.9 dp, inside its half row of (334 − 4) / 2 = 165 dp less 16 dp padding.

**What this decides.** At the widest meaning the bar keeps about 30 % of today's width. Whether that is "too narrow to drag" is the owner's call: "that change of the approved layout is the owner's call (Decision 16), not this document's". So Task 7 opens with a STOP. The orchestrator sends the owner this measurement and one question, and Task 7 is built for the answer: INLINE (the approved default) or CAPTION (the alternative).      * ([ROW_CHARS]). When both sides do not fit, each is cut to an even

     * rest to the other. The empty state's tail is never cut: the pad's
     * name gives way and its tag stays. [partnerName] is [partnerName]'s.

        if (partnerName == null) return cutName(mine, ROW_CHARS - NO_PARTNER_TAIL.length) + NO_PARTNER_TAIL

            partnerName.length <= room - room / 2 -> room - partnerName.length to partnerName.length

     * fits, the tag stands alone. A name with no tag (a room, a file) is
     * cut at the end.

            val rest = name.substring(m.value.length).trimStart()

            return if (keep < 1 || head.isEmpty()) m.value else "${head.take(keep).trimEnd()}… ${m.value}"
        }
        return name.take(max - 1).trimEnd() + "…"
