# The pedal board, benchmarked: two filter-and-modulation plug-ins, and what the rack takes from them

**Status:** benchmark, 2026-10-21, nothing decided or built. A luthier pass over the two plug-in manuals the owner supplied: Devious Machines, *Infiltrator 2* (the maker's 50-page manual, read in full, plus three published reviews for what it leaves out) and Polyverse Music, *Filterverse* (the maker's 137-page manual, v1.2.0, read in full: structure, filter section, modulation, the filter reference and the modulation-source appendix). Repo read at commit `565551e7`.
**Builds on:** the pedal board spec (`2026-10-04-pedal-board-design.md`, Stages 0 to 5), the WAH section (`2026-10-17-wah-section-design.md`, built, gate pending) and the brainstorm of the next pedals (`2026-10-19-pedal-board-brainstorm.md`). Nothing here replaces a numbered stage; the proposals slot in beside them the way the brainstorm's lettered stages do.
**Who this is for:** the owner, then an implementing session. Every proposal says what it reuses, what it costs, what it would be measured by, and what the owner would listen for.

## 1. The two tools, as mechanism

**Infiltrator 2.** Ten effect slots in series, each holding one of about 54 algorithms (four analogue-modelled filter families plus notch, dual and formant; comb, phaser, pitch shift, frequency shift, ring mod, FM, distortion, decimator, delay, reverb, early reflections, vocoder, loop, stretch, varispeed, slow, reverse; EQ; gain, pan, gate, compress; fourteen FFT effects). Every slot has the same modulation kit: envelope 1 is always wired to the slot's main knob and cycles as an LFO at a synced or free rate, or fires once on an audio peak or a MIDI note; a secondary modulator (envelope 2 or an audio level follower with attack, hold, decay and gate) goes to any of the slot's parameters; the envelope is a drawn, grid-snapped, loopable shape with step editing, curve types, swing, random, quantise, invert and pitch snap to a scale. A 32-step sequencer switches slots on and off per step at a chosen speed, with loop region, rotate and randomise. A master panel outside the chain holds a drive (placed before or after the whole chain by one toggle), a compressor, Clean Bass (everything under a set frequency bypasses the whole chain), output gain and a wet/dry mix with a selectable crossfade law. Four macros (eight on demand) each move several parameters with a per-parameter range and curve. (Manual pp. 10, 14 to 47.)

**Filterverse.** Three filter slots, each holding one of about 33 "filters" in five families: classic (a saturating state-variable filter, a saturating ladder, a Sallen-Key with per-pole "damage", a Butterworth to 16 poles, a linear Chebyshev brick wall, a linear EQ and a linear transparent filter), multi-peak (two band-passes with one inverted, an asymmetric notch, a vowel mouth with lips and tongue, Chebyshev ripples, a double-slit interference pair), all-pass phasers (plain, contrary dual, fractally nested), combs (a flanger with feedback morphing and phase-corrected damping, an inverted-feedback tube, a three-string waveguide, nested combs, a twelve-line chorus with golden-ratio phase spacing), and non-filters (a reverb whose size is the cutoff, a spring of summed combs, a decimator with ten interpolators, a Shepard-tone and frequency-shifting phaser with peaks at semitone intervals, a cabinet with a size knob, four tempo delays, a granular delay). The three slots sit in one of five routing schemes (serial, parallel, three hybrids) and can be dragged into any order; each has its own input gain (plus 18 dB, meant for driving the saturating models), output gain, wet/dry, polarity flip, pan and mid-side. The cutoff knob is a pitch: semitones around a root note, with note entry, octave jumps and a tuning table. Eight modulation slots take any of eight sources: a meta knob with scale quantise and glide, an ADSR, a sequencer-LFO (100 patterns, 16 editable steps, a five-way shape morph from rounded through sample-and-hold to per-step drops, swing, retrigger, phase rotate), an audio-rate oscillator (filter FM, with a one-shot "ping" mode), an envelope follower that is either a full-time follower or a threshold-triggered attack-release envelope, MIDI and CV, a random generator on tempo divisions with scale quantise, and a pitch detector so the cutoff tracks the input's note. Sources cross-modulate; modulation depth can differ per stereo side. (Manual pp. 18 to 39, 58 to 131.)

## 2. The behaviours that define them, and what each means here

As mechanisms. For each: what it does better than anything in the roster, and the SnipSnap constraint that changes the adaptation (offline, mono, one pad at a time, 3 to 6 macros plus AMT, motion bakes into the render, the rack has no tempo and the KEYED family does, every section is peak-matched).

### 2.1 Infiltrator 2

The missing half is the curve: one field on `MacroSpec`. |

### 2.2 Filterverse

| | Behaviour (mechanism) | Better than the roster at | What changes here |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **The cutoff is a pitch.** The knob reads in semitones about a root note, accepts note names, jumps by octaves; a modulation depth of 12 semitones makes the cutoff track MIDI or the detected pitch; the meta knob and the random source quantise to scales (pp. 32, 54, 111, 125, 130). | A filter that sits on a note, a sweep that lands on scale degrees, a resonance that follows the melody. The rack's filters are in hertz (WAH heel 280 Hz, toe 2800 Hz; CONTOUR 80 Hz to 16 kHz; `Wah.kt:41-42`, `Contour.kt`). | The house knows two pitches the tool must guess: the pad's own (`Pitch.detect(snip, fromSec, windowSec)` in `:audio`, offline, one pass) and the kit's key (the KEYED family). | B2 | **Nonlinear by default.** Most classic models saturate inside the loop; each topology's saturation does something different to the resonance (the SVF keeps the bass and widens the peak; the ladder gives the bass up for the peak; the Sallen-Key has six per-pole characters and a "damage" slider; the 16-pole Butterworth pitch-shifts when modulated). Two models are linear on purpose, and the manual tells you which (pp. 59 to 68). The 18 dB input boost exists to drive them. | A filter with a character, not a curve: the wah "quack" of real pedals lives in the loop's nonlinearity. | The house SVF already has it: `Dsp.TptSvf.process(..., saturate = true)` puts a tanh on both integrator states (`Dsp.kt:252-253`); VELVET uses it, and the KDoc records that FATHOM, PadFilter and WOBBLE failed their own tests with it on. WAH is linear by decision ("nothing to oversample"). The constraint that changes the adaptation: the rack peak-matches every section, so "level into the filter" can never be a tone control here; drive has to be a macro of the section, with its own 4x story as VALVE's. |
| B3 | **The type is a slider, not a switch.** Low-pass, notch, band-pass, peak, high-pass as one continuous morph; poles as a crossfade (pp. 60, 61, 68). | One knob walks the filter from dark to hollow to thin; a wah that is a low-pass at the heel and a band-pass at the toe. | The TPT SVF gives `low`, `band` and `high` on the same sample (`Dsp.kt:209`), so a morph is a crossfade of three outputs that already exist. WAH uses `band` only plus 0.05 dry (`Wah.DRY`). |
| B4 | **Three slots, five routings, per-slot polarity.** Serial, parallel and three hybrids; drag to reorder; a phase-flip per filter so "mixing in the dry signal and controlling the polarity" makes new filter models (pp. 27 to 30); and several multi-peak models are themselves two filters in parallel with one inverted (Dueling Bands, The Valley, Deep Phase). | Parallel and difference filters: a notch from two band-passes, a resonant pair that cancels to a needle. | The rack's order is fixed on purpose ("no routing screen, no wrong answers") and stays so. Parallel lives *inside* a section: a two-filter section with a polarity is one section in one slot, which is exactly how the tool builds its own multi-peak models. |
| B5 | **The follower has a split personality.** Threshold at minimum: a full-time envelope follower. Threshold above it: an attack-release envelope fired when the input crosses it (pp. 117, 118). | One source covers "with the hit" and "once per hit" without a second module. | This is the cleanest shape for the Moves kit: one TOUCH source with a threshold dial, 0 = follower (today's TOUCH), above 0 = a gesture restarted at each onset over the threshold. It replaces the separate "retrigger" of A3 and the brainstorm never had to name it. |
| B6 | **The sequencer is also the LFO, with a shape morph.** 100 patterns from a sine upward, 16 editable steps, a shape slider from rounded through linear, sample-and-hold, per-step drops and curved drops; swing; phase rotate; rate as a division or in hertz (pp. 119 to 122). | One source is sine LFO, stepped sequence and rhythmic "pluck" envelope by one slider. | Offline and with no phone editor, a shape morph is one macro and a pattern is a CLI dial; the per-step drop (the signal falls to zero after each step) is the trance-gate filter groove the roster lacks, and it is the knob-driven twin of A2's send-switching. |
| B7 | **Audio-rate modulation and the one-shot ping.** An oscillator at audio rate on the cutoff (filter FM); a one-shot mode that plays one cycle "for drum design and filter pinging" (pp. 126 to 128). | Filter FM, and a resonant filter struck once. | A struck resonant filter is the house's BODY and TIDE's low-pass gate: it exists. Filter FM does not; it is not a pedal and the guitar audience does not want it. Noted, not proposed. |
| B8 | **Vowels as two axes.** Lips open-to-closed and tongue front-to-back place a four-filter mouth on the IPA grid (p. 73, the table), with a per-formant drive. | Every vowel from two continuous controls, no vowel list. | The brainstorm's TALK proposed a follower-driven vowel; this is the macro law it was missing: LIPS and TONGUE as two 0..1 macros, and a move on either. VOX and MURMUR already hold formant tables on the engine side. |
| B9 | **Peaks at musical intervals, and the endless rise.** The Shepard-tone phaser spaces its resonant peaks in semitones and rotates them up or down in a tempo-synced cycle with no reset (pp. 95 to 97). | A phaser that is in tune, and a motion that never arrives. | A motion with no reset is periodic by construction, and a LOOP pad is periodic by contract (`Keys.seamError`): a rise whose cycle equals the loop length is seamless for free. The roster has no endless gesture in the rack. A frequency shifter needs a Hilbert pair the repo lacks (grep: none). |
| B10 | **Physics and instruments as filters.** A three-string waveguide (position, damp, inharmonic timbre, detune), a spring as summed detuned combs, a reverb whose size is the cutoff, a cabinet with a formant-shifting size knob, a chorus of twelve lines whose LFO phases are spaced by the inverse square of the golden ratio to kill beating (pp. 83 to 90, 98). | The pad passes *through* an instrument. | The house owns the instruments already: `Strings.Loop` (the SAY become-strung design, `2026-09-30-become-strung-say-design.md`, is this idea), SPRING, CHAMBER, VALVE's cabinet (two-pole wall law, `Valve.kt:111-113`), ENSEMBLE's six players with their own vibrato phases (`EnsemblePlayers.kt:68`). What transfers is parameter laws, not mechanisms: the golden-ratio phase spacing, the cabinet SIZE, the cascading-interval feedback. |

**Dropped from both, with the constraint that drops it:** stereo (pan, width, ping-pong, mid-side, per-side modulation depth: the rack is mono except ENSEMBLE's widening); MIDI, CV, sidechain, DAW sync, phase lock, latency and quality menus (no host, no transport); the envelope editor, the 32-step grid, the 16-step pattern editor and the routing matrix as UI (no per-section dials on the phone; the CLI is the editor; the order is fixed); preset browsers, tags, dice (the house has `scramble` and the seeded `Shuffle` bank, and authors presets by ear after a gate); the vocoder (VOX and MURMUR are engines); the 14 FFT effects as a set (two are new here, G1); filter FM (B7).

**What neither tool can do that the house does:** render a pad deterministically byte for byte; refuse, in words, a pad too short for the pattern; know the pad's root and the kit's key and tempo without a host; bake a move so the MPC plays it with no plug-in; keep an echo's tail when the pattern switches the pedal off; make a loop pad's motion seamless by matching its period to the loop.

## 3. The family synthesis, and the composite

The two tools are one family: *a filter (or any effect) with a main knob, and a kit of ways to move it.* The shared core is that kit: follower, clock, one-shot gesture, step pattern, random, a macro. The house keeps **one implementation** of it (F1), where today it has one private copy inside WAH.

Unique to the first tool and composing (each on a different axis): switching a slot's *send* on a step grid (when a pedal is heard); the low band bypassing the chain (which frequencies reach it); the amp before or after the chain as one toggle; the macro curve. Unique to the second and composing: the knob's *units* (a pitch, snapped to the key); the knob-driven step pattern with per-step drops (the same grid as the send switch, a different destination); the follower's threshold (one source, two personalities); the type morph (which filter) and the in-loop saturation (what character), both inside a section; two-axis vowels as a macro law. Competing at the same stage, where one is chosen: in-loop saturation versus WAH's linear decision (after the gate, as a macro with neutral 0, never silently); the tool's routing matrix versus the fixed order (the order wins; parallel goes inside a section); the per-slot mix versus AMT (AMT wins, with a law per macro).

**The composite: the pedal board that moves, in tune.** One design, one macro story.

```
 THE PAD (dry, from the bin; its root from Pitch.detect, its key and tempo from the kit)
   |
   +--> KEEP LOW: one-pole low-pass at F ----------------------------------------+  (F3, a chain door)
   |         low = lp(x); high = x - low  (sums back to x exactly)                |
   v                                                                              |
 high --> the rack in its fixed order (… contour -> wah -> squash -> valve …)      |
          a section's main knob may now MOVE (F1, the Moves kit lifted from WAH): |
            pos(t) = TOUCH*p_touch(t) + AUTO*p_auto(t) + ROCK*p_rock(t) + STEP*p_step(t)
            TOUCH has a threshold: 0 = follower, >0 = the gesture restarts per onset (B5)
            the knob's units may be semitones about the pad's root (B1, T4/T5)
            WAH may gain TYPE (lp..bp..hp, B3) and DRIVE (the SVF's tanh, B2) as macros
   |                                                                              |
   v                                                                              |
 wet ----------------------------------------------------------+                  |
                                                               |                  |
 STEPS (keyed, reads the kit's bpm and division) --------------+--> out(t) = s(t) ? wet : dry
   s(t) from a pattern string on the grid, 3 ms raised-cosine edges (GATE's);     |
```

**Clone check.** The first tool has no pitch units, no in-loop saturation, no parallel-with-polarity, no pad root. The second has no slot sequencer, no clean bass, no drive toggle. Neither bakes per pad, refuses a short pad, sequences the send and keeps the tail, or snaps to the kit's key. The house does not get their envelope editors, their routing matrices, their ten or three free slots or their stereo. That is the trade, named.

### 3.1 The macro story

Nothing new for the novice to learn on the pad sheet. A chip is still one tap; AMT still means "how much"; the moves are weights inside recipes; the pattern, the threshold and the keep-low frequency are CLI dials first (`Keyed.Dials`, the way WOBBLE's division is) and sheet doors only after a gate says they are worth a chip. Macros stay 0..1 with a neutral where they do nothing, as the rack contract requires.

| Control | Lives in | Default / neutral | What it changes in the model | Proves it moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TOUCH, AUTO, ROCK, STEP | any section that adopts the Moves kit (today only WAH) | 0 / 0 | the weight of each move on the section's main knob | the WAH tests, copied per section: centroid swing, period, open-then-close; STEP: the knob at each step's value to the frame outside the edges |
| threshold (dial) | the Moves kit, on TOUCH | 0 | 0 = follower; above it, the gesture restarts at each onset over the threshold (hysteresis 6 dB, hold-off 40 ms as the first tool's defaults, then measured) | on a two-hit loop, two gestures; on a one-shot, bit-identical to TOUCH |
| SPEED | the Moves kit | 0.5 / 0.5 | the LFO rate, the follower's release, the rock's length together (WAH's law) | WAH's SPEED tests |
| TYPE (WAH) | `Wah.MACROS` | 0.5 / 0.5 | the crossfade of the SVF's `low`, `band`, `high` outputs: 0 low-pass, 0.5 band-pass (today's wah), 1 high-pass | centroid at the three stops; bit-identical at 0.5 |
| DRIVE (WAH) | `Wah.MACROS` | 0 / 0 | the SVF's `saturate` with an input gain law, 4x only when driven (VALVE's rule) | THD on a steady sine rises monotonically; bit-identical at 0; the aliasing probe |
| pattern (dial) | `Keyed.Dials`, CLI `treat` | none | the step string on the kit's division, for STEPS (the send) or STEP (the knob) | dry on off-steps and wet on on-steps, to the frame |
| keep-low (dial) | the chain door | off / off | the crossover under which the pad bypasses the rack | the output's low band within 1e-6 of the dry pad's |
| fade law (per macro) | `MacroSpec`, read by `Treatments.fade` | linear | how AMT walks a macro toward its neutral (log for a gain) | THD or gain versus AMT roughly linear in dB |

## 4. Findings, ranked

Each: the observation with its source, why it matters for the sound or the architecture, what to try, and what it costs. "Measured" here means a grep on the named commit; no audio was rendered for this document.

### Fix now

**F1. The moves are both tools' whole modulation story, and they are private to one section.** `Wah.kt` lines 82 to 124 hold `touchAt`, `autoAt`, `rockAt`, `touchPositions` and `reach` as `internal` members of `Wah` (verified). The first tool wires the same modulators to every slot's main knob; the second has eight slots of them for three filters. The house has them once, in one file. This is the recurring finding "a private helper copied between engines is where the next toolkit lives". Try: lift them into a `Moves` object in `synth` (the position law, the reach stretch, the follower with the second tool's threshold (B5), the gesture, a STEP pattern source (B6), the one-cycle floor the brainstorm's SHORT PAD wants), keep WAH bit-identical (its tests pin the sweep's numbers), then let the staged TREMOLO be "AUTO on a gain", the staged FLANGER's and VIBRATO's LFO be the same `autoAt`, CHOKE's close be "TOUCH on a gain, inverted", and the brainstorm's VIBRATO "LATE" be an arrival ramp in the kit (the second tool's cross-modulation example: an envelope fades in the LFO). Cost: a refactor with no audible change, S; the win is that Stage 4's two sections and the brainstorm's VIBRATO, CHOKE and TALK stop writing their own LFO and follower. Hazard: WAH's goldens; do it before Stage 4, not after.

**F2. AMT conflates "how much pedal" with "which setting", and both tools keep them apart.** Per-slot Mix (first tool p. 28; second tool p. 30, per filter) is an output crossfade; the main knob is the setting. `Treatments.fade` (verified, `Treatments.kt`) is a parameter fade, so a hot amp at AMT 0.7 is a different amp, not the same amp quieter: the Stage 0 "not enough" and the spec's "20 to 25 times hotter at 1 than at 0.7" are both this. Two honest answers, pick one per recipe: (a) author values so 0.7 reads full (the WAH's stretch, the brainstorm's section 7 question 7), or (b) a fade law per macro (A6): `MacroSpec` gains an optional law (`LINEAR`, `LOG`), and `fade` walks a LOG macro in the section's own gain domain, so DRIVE at 0.7 is 70 percent of the way to gain 1000 in dB, not gain 11. (b) is one field, one `when`, one test (THD versus AMT on a 220 Hz sine, monotone and roughly linear in dB) and it does not touch the contract "AMT 0 lands on every neutral". It also answers the brainstorm's FULL TAP without the dead zone. Cost: S. A wet/dry crossfade is declined: it would break "AMT 0 is the neutral" and the peak-match contract.

**F3. KEEP LOW: the low end bypasses the chain.** Clean Bass (first tool pp. 40, 41): everything under a set frequency passes unaffected; the reviewer's use is a kick through a drum-bus mangling. The rack has nothing like it (grep for crossover, split, keep: only Tonewheel's rotary crossover and SMEAR's FLOOR band, verified); a DRIVE chip on a kick drives the sub, and the brainstorm's SUB notes a phone speaker cannot even hear the result. Try: a chain door, not a section: `low = OnePole.lp(x, F)`, `high = x - low`, the rack runs on `high`, `low` is added back after it, then the usual peak match. A first-order complementary pair sums back to the input exactly (by construction, `high + low = x`), so there is no Linkwitz-Riley crossover to design and no phase hazard at the seam; the price is a 6 dB per octave slope, which for "keep the thump" is the right trade. Lands in `Treatments.apply` beside the pre-amp fade, so the chip, the CLI `treat` and the recorded character all get it; `PadRecipe.process` does not (the same disclosed edge as the fade). Dial: a frequency, CLI first (120 Hz default, the tool's), a sheet door later. Hazards: VALVE normalises to its input's peak, and the high band alone peaks lower, so the amp drives harder than before at the same AMT (measure THD with and without); a pad whose whole energy is under F is passed untouched and the chip appears to do nothing (say so in words, the ROLL and GATE way). Gate: a kick through DRIVE at AMT 1 with keep-low on and off; a kick through WAH; the choir (unchanged above 120 Hz). Cost: about 40 lines plus tests, S.

### Try next

**T1. STEPS: the pattern, landed in the KEYED family, in two forms.** The first tool's 32-step on/off per slot (pp. 30, 31) and the second's 16-step sequencer with per-step drops on the knob (pp. 119 to 122) are the same grid with two destinations. Form one, the send: a keyed treatment `stepped` whose dial is a pattern string on the kit's division (`Keyed.Dials.pattern`, "x.x.xx.." on `Wobble.DIVISIONS`), AMT its depth, GATE's 3 ms raised-cosine edges and GATE's refusal (a pad shorter than two divisions). It needs two renders, dry and wet, so it is a `KitBuilder` composition (the sheet already restores the bin's original before a chip, and `rewriteEveryFile` is the door), the same shape as the brainstorm's MERGE; the offline win is that the pattern switches the *input* to the tail-making sections (ECHO, SPRING, DUB), so a sequenced-off echo rings out instead of cutting. Form two, the knob: STEP as a fourth weight in the Moves kit, the pattern's values driving the section's main knob with the second tool's drop shape (the value falls to the heel after each step), which on WAH is the trance-gate filter groove. Gate: the choir with WAH on "x.x.x.xx": does the wah come and go on the beat, and does the spring tail survive the off-steps? A JANGLE chord with DRIVE on "xx..xx..": a rhythm, or a chopped guitar? Hazards: the one-second tail cap across a pattern of many on-steps; the pattern bakes at the tempo it was treated at (the WOBBLE rule); the KEYED row has two free chips and PUMP, SHIMMER and HOWL want them. Cost: M (form one), S after F1 (form two). The owner has not asked for this; it is the one thing in either tool the roster cannot approach, so it is the first thing to listen to from this benchmark.

**T2. The follower's threshold, on loop pads.** The second tool's follower is a full-time follower at threshold 0 and a triggered attack-release envelope above it (pp. 117, 118); the first tool's audio retrigger adds hysteresis and hold-off (pp. 38, 39). On a one-shot nothing changes: TOUCH is the hit. On a LOOP or HOLD pad, or a chopped break, each onset over the threshold restarts the gesture, so a two-bar loop through WAH rocks on every snare. The `:audio` onset detector exists (`Chopper.byTransients`); the first tool's defaults (6 dB hysteresis, 40 ms hold-off) are starting values to measure. One dial on TOUCH in the Moves kit (F1 first), not a new weight. Gate: a drum loop through WAH with the threshold up: one wah per hit, no double triggers on a flam? Cost: S after F1.

**T3. The amp's position is one toggle, not a routing screen.** The first tool's only routing freedom is Drive before or after the whole chain (p. 24); the second's five schemes exist, and its own multi-peak models show the better answer: parallel paths live *inside* one filter (B4). Together they are evidence for Stage 3's rule: "this pedal before the amp or after it", decided per pedal by its slot, and nothing more. Nothing to build.

**T4. The wah's centre in semitones, and a wah that lands on notes.** The second tool's whole premise (B1): the cutoff is a pitch. Two steps. First, in the rack: express the WAH's heel and toe as semitones about the pad's detected root (`Pitch.detect`, one offline pass, as the peak is measured today), so the sweep spans the same interval on every note and the resonance sits on a harmonic rather than on 280 Hz; unpitched pads keep hertz and the diagnostics say which path ran. Second, in the KEYED family (the first tool's pitch snap, the second's scale quantise): a keyed form of WAH whose centre path is quantised to scale degrees of the kit's key (`Scales.nearestInKey`), holding each for a division, which is the arpeggiated filter; and a seeded random walk on the same grid (the second tool's random generator, deterministic here via `Dsp.seedFor`). Gate: the choir through it in a keyed kit: an arpeggiated filter, or a wah with steps? A JANGLE note: does the quack sit on the chord? Cost: S each after F1 and T1. Parked until WAH passes its gate, because it changes the numbers the gate hears.

**T5. WAH gains TYPE.** The second tool's type slider (B3): low-pass to band-pass to high-pass as one morph. The house SVF gives `low`, `band` and `high` on every sample (`Dsp.kt:209`, verified), so TYPE is a crossfade of outputs WAH already computes: 0 low-pass (the dark wah, a cocked-wah tone at the heel), 0.5 band-pass (today, bit-identical), 1 high-pass (thin, the "telephone" sweep). One macro, neutral and default 0.5, so nothing moves until a recipe sets it. Hazard: the peak match across types (a low-pass passes far more energy than a band-pass, so the match re-levels the wet); `Wah.DRY` 0.05 may want to differ per type. Gate: the three stops on the choir and JANGLE. Cost: S, after the WAH gate.

**T6. WAH gains DRIVE, from a flag the house already has.** The second tool's filters saturate inside the loop by default (B2) and the manual says the character of each is in how its saturation treats the resonance. `Dsp.TptSvf.process(..., saturate = true)` is a tanh on both integrator states (`Dsp.kt:252-253`, verified); VELVET runs with it, and the KDoc records that three other callers failed their own tests with it on, so it is a decision per section, not a default. The WAH spec chose linear ("nothing to oversample"), rightly, for a first gate. After the gate, a DRIVE macro (neutral 0: bit-identical to today) with an input gain law into the saturating SVF, oversampled 4x only when driven (VALVE's measured rule), would be the "more character" the review rubric asks after. Measured first, never heard first: THD versus DRIVE on a steady sine, the aliasing probe, and the resonance peak's position with and without (the second tool says the SVF's saturation shifts it). Gate: the owner's "extreme enough" at DRIVE 1 on a snare, the choir, both guitars. Cost: M (the 4x path), S for the linear-only flag. Declined as a default: the owner has not heard the linear one yet.

**T7. Vowels as two axes, for TALK.** The second tool's mouth (B8, p. 73) places four filters by LIPS (open to closed) and TONGUE (front to back), and the manual's table maps the nine combinations to IPA vowels. The brainstorm's TALK ranked 12th partly because its macro story was a follower on an unnamed vowel path; this is the law it lacked: LIPS and TONGUE as two 0..1 macros, a move on either (TOUCH on LIPS is "the mouth opens with the hit"), VOX's formant tables as the source of the four centres. Still after WAH, still gated on "a mouth, not a wah with company". Cost unchanged (M).

### Big idea

**G1. Two spectral effects the roster lacks, in a rack where latency is free.** The first tool's FFT set (pp. 21 to 24) pays in latency (its manual warns, its reviewers advise delay compensation). Offline, that cost is zero, and `Pghi` exists in `:audio` (ETERNAL uses it). Most of the fourteen are covered or out of audience. Two are new here and cheap on top of PGHI: **Corrupt** (keep only the loudest or only the quietest partials, by an amount) and **Lofi** (drop a fraction of harmonics, re-chosen at a rate). Both are the novice's "make it broken" tap and neither serves the guitar audience, so they wait behind the pedal board. Cost: M each, a 1-second tail story, the PGHI phase care.

**G2. A board is a macro with curves.** Once F2 exists per macro, a board (the brainstorm's RIG, WALL, BOOM) can carry a law per member, which both tools give per parameter (first tool p. 43; second tool p. 37, "more resolution between zero and the 25 percent mark, then linear"). This is the brainstorm's SOUNDS row made coherent at every AMT, with no FULL TAP. Decide after F2 has been heard on DRIVE.

**G3. The endless rise, for loop pads.** The second tool's Shepard phaser (B9) rotates interval-spaced peaks up or down with no reset. A LOOP pad is periodic by contract, and a rise whose cycle is the loop's length (or an integer division of it) is seamless by construction: the one kind of motion that survives HOLD without a seam test. Lands as a keyed treatment on LOOP pads only (it reads the loop length), after PHASE in spirit. A frequency shifter needs a Hilbert pair the repo lacks and is not proposed. Cost: M. Gate: a HOLD pad played eight times end to end: does it rise forever without a hiccup?

**G4. Three parameter laws worth a line each.** From B10: (a) ENSEMBLE's players carry their own vibrato phases (`EnsemblePlayers.kt:68`); spacing those phases by the inverse square of the golden ratio (about 0.382 of a turn apart) is the second tool's way to kill beating periodicity in a chorus, one constant, measured by the autocorrelation of the wet's envelope; (b) VALVE's cabinet as a SIZE law (shift the wall's corners in parallel, p. 98): VALVE's goldens are pinned at 1e-5, so only with a neutral equal to today's size, and only if a listen asks for a bigger box; (c) SHIMMER's octave-in-the-feedback generalised to any interval (the second tool's pitch-in-the-feedback delay, p. 105, "the pitch change accumulates with each repetition"): a fifth cascading is a different pedal from an octave cascading, one dial on the brainstorm's SHIMMER when it comes.

### Disclosed, not fixed

- **Nothing in this document was rendered.** The numbers quoted are the tools' (their manuals) and the repo's own measured ones (the pedal board spec, the WAH spec, VALVE's and the SVF's KDoc). The first measurement to take is F3's: a kick through DRIVE with and without keep-low, THD and the sub band's deviation.
- **The pattern's tempo bakes.** T1 renders at the kit's BPM at the time of the tap, as WOBBLE, ROLL and GATE do; a later tempo change does not re-render. The house has accepted this for the keyed family and should say so again in T1's spec.
- **The KEEP LOW door is not the door for every consumer** (BREED's children, a patch recipe's own render, the remix bank run the bare chain), the same edge the pre-amp fade carries and Stage 3 must decide.
- **Semitone units need a root.** T4's first step changes what the WAH gate hears on pitched pads (the heel and toe move with the note). It is parked for that reason, and a snare keeps hertz: the diagnostics must say which path a render took, or the two paths become "one quantity in two places".
- **The second tool's saturating SVF flag has failed three callers' tests** (`Dsp.kt` KDoc). T6 is a measured decision per section, never a default flip.

## 8. Why it is built this way

- **The moves become a toolkit before anything else** because both tools' single shared idea is that one modulation kit fits every effect, and the house already wrote that kit once, inside WAH. The second tool even hands over the cleanest shape for it (a follower with a threshold is both "with the hit" and "once per hit"). Copying it into TREMOLO and FLANGER would be the private-helper defect the review rubric looks for; lifting it first costs a refactor and buys every later pedal its motion for free.
- **AMT gets a law per macro rather than a wet/dry knob** because the rack's contract says AMT 0 is the neutral and every section is peak-matched; a crossfade would break both and the tests that pin them. A log law keeps the contract and removes the one place AMT lies (an amp's gain).
- **KEEP LOW is a door, not a section,** because it has to see the whole chain and a first-order split sums back exactly, so the cheapest version is also the correct one. The risky decision is the amp: with the sub removed the amp sees a lower peak and drives harder, which is a measurement to take before the gate, not a reason not to build it.
- **The wah's new macros all have a neutral at today's sound** (TYPE 0.5, DRIVE 0, hertz until a root is found) because the WAH gate has not happened yet; a benchmark must not move the thing the owner is about to judge. The second tool's whole roster says character lives in the loop's saturation, and the house already has the flag, so the question is only when, and the answer is after the gate and after a THD and aliasing measurement, never as a default flip (three callers have already failed with it on).
- **The pattern lives in the KEYED family and switches the send,** because the rack has no tempo and the keyed family already refuses pads too short for a grid in words; and because rendering offline lets the echo's tail ring through an off-step, which neither real-time tool can do. That, and a loop pad's period making the endless rise seamless, are the two places the house can be better than the references rather than a port of them.
- **The one risky decision** is building STEPS at all: the owner has not asked for sequenced effects, and the audience that taps it first is the novice with a loop, not the guitarist. It is ranked first among the "try next" items because it is the one behaviour in either manual the roster cannot reach by any recipe, and a single gate on the choir settles it.

## As built: F2, the per-macro fade law (2026-10-22, `synth` only)

- **The law** is a field on `MacroSpec` (`law: FadeLaw = FadeLaw.LINEAR`; `FadeLaw` is `LINEAR` or `LOG`). VALVE's DRIVE is the one LOG macro. A LOG macro names its gain law on its section in the chain table (`FxChain.Section.gains`, read through `FxChain.gainOf`); VALVE's is `Valve.gainFor`. `Treatments.fade` walks a LOG macro so that its gain is the AMT's fraction of the way from the neutral's gain (DRIVE 0, gain 0.05) to the character's (DRIVE 1, gain 1000) in decibels, and finds the knob by bisection on `gainFor` (60 halvings), so the gain law stays in one place. The ends are exact (the neutral at AMT 0, the character at 1). Every other macro walks linearly, byte for byte as before; `Treatments.linearFade` keeps the old walk for the tests and the listen and is not called by the product.
- **What moved:** only chains that fade VALVE's DRIVE: `driven` (the DRIVE chip) and `amped` below AMT 1, and anything built from them (a DRIVE or RIG pick on a pedal board). At AMT 1 nothing moved. `amped` (DRIVE 0.6) sits on V1's exponential part of the gain law, where a linear walk of the knob is already a linear walk of the gain in dB, so it moves only by floating-point rounding.
- **Measured** (`./gradlew :synth:generateFadeLawAudition` prints this; `driven` on a steady 220 Hz sine at peak 0.5, THD over harmonics 2 to 8):

| AMT | LOG: DRIVE, gain, THD | old linear: DRIVE, gain, THD |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.151, 0.13, 0.20 % | 0.100, 0.10, 0.10 % |
| 0.3 | 0.454, 0.98, 7.08 % | 0.300, 0.36, 1.28 % |
| 0.5 | 0.668, 7.07, 33.74 % | 0.500, 1.32, 10.46 % |
| 0.7 | 0.801, 51.25, 38.63 % | 0.700, 11.34, 35.99 % |
| 0.9 | 0.934, 371.45, 38.39 % | 0.900, 224.65, 38.38 % |
| 1.0 | 1.000, 1000, 38.05 % | the same |

- **The finding the table shows:** on a steady tone the tube's THD saturates near 38 percent once its gain passes about 20, so at the sheet's AMT 0.7 the law raises the gain into the tube about 13 dB (51 against 11) but steady-tone THD only from 36.0 to 38.6 percent. Where the law changes a steady tone most is the lower half of the stepper (AMT 0.5: 33.7 against 10.5 percent), and the cost is that AMT 0.6 to 1 now sound alike on a sustained note. On a decaying hit the extra gain keeps the tail saturated for longer, which THD on a sine does not measure; that is what the listen is for.
- **The pre-amp end fade** follows the amp's lift, so it lengthened with the law: 54 ms at AMT 0.7 (57 percent of the full 95 ms, where the old walk gave 33), and the amp now starts to lift (gain above 1) at AMT about 0.30 instead of 0.46. The bars the fade was chosen against (a snare and a choir ending at least 53 dB under their peaks with a DC mean under 1e-4, at AMT 0.7 to 1) pass unchanged; the test's window on the fade's length was re-pinned from 10..40 ms to 40..70 ms with the reason.
- **Tests:** `FadeLawTest` (VALVE DRIVE is the only LOG macro and every LOG macro names a positive, rising gain law, and no linear macro declares one; the LOG walk is linear in dB within 0.01 dB at nineteen AMTs and exact at the ends; every non-LOG macro in every character at every AMT equals the linear walk, and a chain with no LOG macro is equal whole; the old walk left `driven` at gain 11.3 at AMT 0.7 and the new one is more than four times that), and in `TreatmentsTest` THD versus AMT on the 220 Hz sine (never falls by more than half a point as AMT rises; at 0.7 the LOG walk is at least a point above the linear one).
- **The listen:** `./gradlew :synth:generateFadeLawAudition` writes `testkit/fade-law-audition/` (gitignored): on a snare, the choir and both MAGNET guitar pads as the sheet bakes them, the dry pad, DRIVE at AMT 70 the old way, DRIVE at AMT 70 the new way, and DRIVE at AMT 100, all at one loudness. The questions: does the new 70 read as the drive pedal you picked at round two, where the old 70 read as "not enough"; and on the choir and the guitars, is the new 70 now too close to 100, so that the top of the stepper does too little?
- **Heard:** the owner, 2026-10-23, on `testkit/fade-law-audition/` (DRIVE at AMT 70 on the snare, the choir and both MAGNET guitar pads): the LOG version is better than the old linear walk; and the new 70 does not sit noticeably close to 100 on the choir and the guitars, the risk the steady-tone THD table raised, so no gentler curve for DRIVE's walk is needed.
