# The DELAY pedal: ECHO gains a HEAD knob

**Status:** design, 2026-10-22, written from the owner's two listens of the pedal board's Stage 1b page and the owner's request "Write the DELAY spec: ECHO gains a HEAD knob"; reviewed adversarially before commit; revised 2026-10-23 with the owner's answers to its four questions (HEAD on, MIX 0.75, the spacing kept, ROLL left where it is), and for the pedal library, which landed on 2026-10-22 (PR #623); built 2026-10-23 from the plan ("As built", at the end), awaiting the owner's gate. The plan follows this document; the DELAY chip joins the pedals only if the gate at the end passes.
**Builds on:** the pedal board spec (`2026-10-04-pedal-board-design.md`), its brainstorm (`2026-10-19-pedal-board-brainstorm.md`, idea 2, DELAY), the pedal library design (`2026-10-21-pedal-library-and-board-design.md`, the owner's "five or six on a pad at once" direction, built as its R1 in PR #623: `Pedals.kt`, `PedalBoard.kt`) and its benchmark (`2026-10-21-pedal-board-benchmark.md`, F2, the fade law), the Stage 1b listens (`PedalListensGenerator`, PR #609 and PR #619), ECHO (`synth/.../Echo.kt`), and ENSEMBLE's SECTION, the precedent for a macro added to a section that already ships.
**Roadmap row:** the pedal board's row, S30 in `docs/SYNTH_ROADMAP.md`; this extends it.

## Why

A delay pedal repeats what goes into it. On a drum hit the repeat lands in silence and is heard as the hit again; on a held note it lands on the note itself, which is still sounding. The owner's first listen of the Stage 1b page found the attack does not come back on the choir and the jangle: "delay doesn't really seem to have the intended effect of choir and jangle. You don't hear the attach repeat/echo like with a short hit." Measured on the page's own clips (PR #619): where the first repeat lands, 225 ms in, the snare is about 46 dB under its hit and the repeat lifts that moment 37 dB; the jangle is only 2 dB under its hit and the repeat moves it half a decibel.

A follow-up sheet put ROLL and three attack-fed echo prototypes beside the plain DELAY. The owner's verdict named the plain DELAY and one prototype together, and asked for that prototype's mechanism as a knob on ECHO. This design builds it.

**Success:** ECHO gains HEAD, and every recipe and saved kit that uses ECHO today renders bit for bit as it does now; one DELAY chip that is a delay on a drum hit and brings a held note's attack back as a repeat you can hear (at the MIX the owner picked, 0.75, or the neighbour the gate picks by ear); the chip passes the owner's gate, or it does not ship.

## What the numbers say

Measured 2026-10-22 with two scratch tests on commit `c5274e0f`, the author's (`ScratchDelayProbe`) and the review's (`ReviewProbe`, which runs the real `attackEcho` and reproduces the shipped ECHO bit for bit at HEAD 0), at the DELAY values, on the four gate sources (the snare, the choir, and both MAGNET guitars as the kit has them, through their landing amps). Neither scratch test is committed; the plan's first step re-creates the probe in test source, and the tests pin the numbers decision 3 rests on. "HEAD on" below is the head-fed feed at the given MIX, with no lift. The choir's mono-fold figures and the tail cap's at MIX 0.75 are a third run of the author's probe, on 2026-10-23 (on `a4518cf9`, whose main source is `c5274e0f`'s), which first reproduced the figures this document already had at MIX 1.0 and for the plain DELAY (the choir's mono fold at both AMTs; the tail cap's 34, 37, 90 and 52 dB). The probe's own copy of the prototype lifts both channels by one gain, so for the liked choir clip it prints 0.2 dB peak and 2.7 rms rather than the real prototype's 0.5 and 3.0 below; the re-created probe must lift each channel on its own, as `attackEcho` does.

**The first repeat's onset is the same either way.** A repeat's first 80 ms (the window less its fade) is the repeat of the note's first 80 ms, which the head feeds whole, so the first repeat's onset, and its peak on these sources, reads the same with HEAD on and off at the same MIX, up to the peak match's one gain (the review measured the two renders identical at 20, 60 and 79 ms into the repeat and diverging from 81 ms). HEAD changes what follows: whether the repeat stops when the head has passed, or keeps repeating the sustained body.

**HEAD makes the repeats discrete.** The rms of the whole output, dry note included, from 10 ms after the head's first repeat ends to 5 ms before the second repeat lands (355 to 445 ms at AMT 100; 281 to 298 ms at AMT 70), against the rms of the first repeat's first 20 ms (225 to 245 ms; 151 to 171 ms), at the recipe's MIX 0.75:

| Source | HEAD off, AMT 100 | HEAD on, AMT 100 | HEAD off, AMT 70 | HEAD on, AMT 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Choir | −0.5 dB | −10.3 dB | −4.6 dB | −9.7 dB |
| Chug | −0.7 dB | −2.4 dB | −0.2 dB | −0.9 dB |
| Jangle | 0.0 dB | −0.7 dB | −0.5 dB | −1.9 dB |
| Snare | −39.9 dB | silence | −36.8 dB | −48.1 dB |

Both levels include the dry note, which keeps ringing under the repeats on the guitars; that is why HEAD moves the guitars by under 2 dB. On the choir at AMT 100, HEAD on's gap sits near −10 dB at every recipe MIX (−9.9 at 0.5, −10.3 at 0.75, −10.8 at 1.0) while HEAD off's rises with MIX (−3.1, −0.5, +1.3), so HEAD's separation there is 6.8, 9.8 and 12.1 dB. At AMT 70 the plain repeats are fainter (MIX 0.525, feedback 0.51), so HEAD off already leaves a gap on the choir and HEAD adds 5.1 dB to it.

**MIX is what lets a guitar's pick through its own body.** The jangle's attack is only 2 dB over the body it sustains into (PR #619), so its repeat has to be loud to be heard. On the jangle: the peak of the first repeat's first 25 ms over the peak of the 20 ms ending 5 ms before it. On the snare: the rms of the first repeat's first 20 ms against the rms of the hit's first 20 ms. HEAD on throughout:

| Recipe MIX | Jangle, AMT 100 | Jangle, AMT 70 | Snare, AMT 100 | Snare, AMT 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 1.8 dB | 2.4 dB | −8.1 dB | −11.3 dB |
| 0.75 (the recipe) | 3.1 dB | 3.5 dB | −4.6 dB | −7.8 dB |
| 1.0 | 4.3 dB | 4.6 dB | −2.1 dB | −5.3 dB |

The liked head clip stood 5.3 dB on the jangle. On the choir, as the page played it (folded to mono), the liked clip measures 0.5 dB peak and 3.0 dB rms by the jangle's metric, against −1.6 and 1.2 at recipe MIX 0.75 at AMT 100, and 0.5 and 1.9 at AMT 70 (at MIX 1.0: −1.3 and 1.8, and 0.8 and 2.1). The plain DELAY the owner passed on the snare (at 70 and 100 on the Stage 1b page, "Everything sounds good") put its first repeat 11.3 and 8.1 dB under the hit. At a matched AMT, recipe MIX 0.75 puts the snare's repeat 3.5 dB hotter than that plain DELAY, about one and a half times its level (MIX 1.0: 6.0 dB, twice). No single MIX reproduces both: the long-note pick wants the repeats hot, and a hot repeat on a drum is a loud, dub-style echo. The owner answered 0.75 from these figures, without a listen.

**AMT 70 is not just quieter.** The pad sheet's first tap fades every macro toward its neutral, so at AMT 70 the repeats are 151 ms apart instead of 225 ms (both liked clips were at 225 ms; the owner kept 151, decision 7), the feedback is 0.51 instead of 0.66, and the loop's low-pass is near 2.0 kHz instead of 2.4. On the jangle, AMT 100 is not louder than 70 (at the recipe's MIX 0.75 the pick stands 3.1 dB at 100 and 3.5 at 70), nor on the choir (1.2 and 1.9 dB rms on the mono fold); the snare gets hotter with AMT (4.6 dB under the hit at 100, 7.8 at 70), and the chug a little (its pick 3.6 dB at 100, 2.5 at 70).

## Decisions taken

1. **A macro on ECHO, not a section.** The owner asked for a HEAD knob on ECHO (the page had offered "a DELAY section with an ATTACK knob", with no stored answer; the request settles it). The head-fed echo is ECHO's loop with one difference, what feeds it; a second section would copy that loop, the one-quantity-in-two-places defect the house keeps finding. HEAD defaults to 0 with neutral 0, and at 0 the loop renders today's samples exactly (below).
2. **HEAD's job is the feed; the level stays MIX's.** The prototype's lift, a gain found per channel from the first repeat's peak, is dropped: it is an input-dependent second level control beside MIX, and with MIX at its top it added 2.0 to 3.8 dB on the clips the owner heard. The recipe sets MIX instead.
3. **The recipe is MIX 0.75, the owner's pick.** Asked "on the drums, MIX 1.0, 0.75 or 0.5?" for the gate (in chat, with the jangle's and the snare's figures at AMT 70), the owner answered "0.75" before it, without a listen. At the pad sheet's default AMT 70 that puts the jangle's pick 3.5 dB over its body (the liked clip, 5.3; MIX 1.0 would give 4.6) and the choir at 1.9 dB rms on the page's mono fold (the liked clip, 3.0; MIX 1.0, 2.1). On drums the snare's first repeat sits 7.8 dB under the hit at 70 and 4.6 under at 100, 3.5 dB hotter than the plain DELAY the owner passed at each AMT. The gate plays MIX 1.0 and 0.5 beside it at AMT 70 on the snare and the jangle, so the pick can move by ear without another round; the pinned bars move with it.
4. **HEAD reaches full at the first tap.** As WAH's weights do (`Wah.reach`), HEAD is stretched by 1.5 and capped at 1, so the recipe's HEAD 1.0 fades to 0.7 at AMT 70 and still feeds only the head. Below AMT about 67 the body starts to feed the repeats again, smoothly.
5. **One library entry, DELAY, with its recipe `delayed`; its chip after the gate.** The pedal library has landed (PR #623), so DELAY is one entry appended at the end of `Pedals.entries` (after RIG): `Entry("DELAY", "delayed", character("delayed"), PedalSide.POST, movable = false, gated = false)`. Its `fade` derives false (it sets no VALVE), and POST is ECHO's own slot's side, which `PedalsTest` requires of a single-section pedal. Until the gate passes it draws no chip and is reachable from the CLI (`treat --pedals delayed`, or `treat` with the character) and from saved kits, the registry's standing rule. Passing the gate flips `gated`; row seven (`PadSheet.PEDAL_SEGMENTS`) and its `CHARACTER_FOR` line are derived from the registry, so no sheet code changes, and the row draws DRIVE, COMP and DELAY (with WAH before DELAY if its gate has passed first), inside the sheet's six-wide limit. ECHO already sits after the amp, where a rig puts a delay, so on a guitar pad the chip on the baked audio is already the rig's order: no dependency on the picker.
6. **ROLL stays where it is.** It is in the app today on the TIME row (a keyed treatment, drawn beside SLAP and GATE), and the owner likes it as "a secondary option". Asked whether that meant leaving it there or a second delay beside DELAY in the pedal library, the owner answered "leave it": nothing is built for it. The brainstorm's tempo-synced delay (the keyed ON BEAT twin) is a separate, later idea.
7. **The spacing stays.** At the first tap the repeats are 151 ms apart, against the 225 ms of both liked clips; offered TIME 1.0 instead (206 ms at AMT 70, 350 at 100), the owner answered "keep" before the gate. The recipe's TIME stays 0.75, and the gate asks about the spacing as the recipe plays it.

## The macro

| Macro | Default | Neutral | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| HEAD | 0 | 0 | how much of the repeats is fed by the note's head alone: 0 repeats the whole note (ECHO today), 1 repeats only the head |

**Constants and helpers** (in `Echo`, `internal` where tests call them): `HEAD_OFF = 0.001f`; `HEAD_SEC = 0.12f`; `reach(macros) = if (HEAD <= HEAD_OFF) 0f else (1.5f * HEAD).coerceIn(0f, 1f)`; `headFrames(delay, rate) = min((HEAD_SEC * rate).toInt(), (0.8f * delay).toInt())`, computed in Float as the prototype does, with the fade `headFrames / 3`. At the recipe's values the delay is 9931 frames and the window 5292 (120 ms, the last 1764 faded: the prototype the owner heard); at TIME 0.375 the delay is 5126 and the window 4100 (93 ms); at TIME 0 the delay is 2646 and the window 2116 (48 ms). The window shrinks with a short TIME so a repeat of the head always ends before the next repeat lands. The 120 ms, the third, the 0.8 and the 1.5 are shape, from the heard prototype, the discrete-repeat rule and WAH's stretch.

**The feed.** With `w(f)` the window (1 inside, the raised-cosine fade, then 0), the bus is fed `x × (1 − reach × (1 − w(f)))` instead of `x`: the whole note at reach 0, the head alone at reach 1. Everything else is ECHO's: one delay, one one-pole low-pass in the loop so each repeat is darker, repeats counted until they fall under −60 dB, the 1.1 s tail ceiling, the dry note added back untouched at full level, MIX scaling the repeats, and the peak match. HEAD does not change the tail's length. So on a held note longer than the head's repeats take to fall away, HEAD on leaves almost no echo after the note ends, while the rack still keeps up to a second of tail: on a held 2 s tone, the second after it ends peaks 77.9 dB under the render at AMT 70 and 35.5 dB under at AMT 100, against 7.7 and 5.1 dB with HEAD off (the build's review, a Python copy of the loop, not committed). The gate asks whether that matters.

**One loop, bit for bit at HEAD 0.** At `HEAD <= HEAD_OFF` the reach is exactly 0f, so the feed factor is exactly 1f (`1f − 0f × (1f − w)` is 1f, and `x × 1f` is `x` for every finite float, -0f included; Kotlin on the JVM rounds each operation separately and fuses nothing). So the existing loop, with that one factor added, renders today's samples bit for bit, and the hash pins below prove it; no second copy of the loop is kept (ENSEMBLE kept a separate path for SECTION; here the only change is a multiply by exactly 1f). The window is the same for every channel and the bus runs per channel, so a stereo pad stays stereo-identical and a mono pad stays mono.

## The recipe

`delayed` = `echo {TIME 0.75, REPEAT 0.85, TONE 0.35, MIX 0.75, HEAD 1.0}`, appended at the end of `Treatments.EXTRA` as it stands at the plan's base (after `rockwahed` on `c5274e0f`). No macro sits on its neutral (all five neutrals are 0). What the pad sheet's default AMT 70 makes of it: repeats 151 ms apart (225 ms at 100), feedback 0.51 (0.66), the loop's low-pass near 2.0 kHz (2.4 kHz), MIX 0.525 (0.75), and HEAD at full through the stretch. The spacing is the owner's (decision 7); the benchmark's fade law (F2) is for gain macros and does not bear on it.

The chip word is DELAY. It appears nowhere on the sheet today (ECHO is SLAP's display word on the TIME row), and no library entry uses it. SLAP is a sheet chip, not a library entry, so it never shares a board with DELAY. The one entry that also sets `echo` is RIG, a board carrying `slapback`'s echo on the AMP side. A later pick wins a section only within its own pass, and RIG and DELAY render in different passes, so a board holding both runs two echoes, RIG's slapback and then DELAY, as a rig with two delay pedals would; the board's one tail budget caps the sum.

- **HEAD on, the contract clauses the shared tests do not reach.** `FxTest` runs determinism, stereo identity and no-widening at each section's defaults, where HEAD is off (only its random rolls, the JSON round trip and AMT 0 reach every macro). So at the recipe's values with HEAD 1: two renders of the snare are identical; a doubled kick and a doubled choir keep identical channels frame by frame; a hard-left pad keeps a silent right channel; a mono pad stays mono.
- **The onset is unchanged by HEAD.** On the choir at the recipe's values, over frames `[0, delay + headFrames − headFrames / 3)`, the HEAD 1 render equals the HEAD 0 render times one constant `k = out1[p] / out0[p]` (p the frame of largest `|out0|` in that range), to 1e-5. The fade and the rest of the repeat are where HEAD acts and are not compared.
- **HEAD makes the repeats discrete.** On the choir at the recipe's values, the gap level defined above with HEAD 1 is at least 7 dB under HEAD 0's (measured 9.8 at the recipe's MIX 0.75: −0.5 to −10.3 dB; the bar sits about 3 dB under the measurement, as the first draft's did). On the wet, HEAD 0 measured −3.7 dB and HEAD 1 under −140 dB; the bars are −6.7 dB (the measurement less 3 dB) and −60 dB.

At TIME 0 with HEAD 1 on a held tone, the wet alone (`(out / k − dry) / mix`, with `k = out / dry` on a frame before the first repeat) is under −60 dB against its own peak over `[delay + headFrames + 2 ms, 2 × delay − 1 ms]` (110 to 119 ms).
- **The stretch.** `reach` is 1f at HEAD 2/3 and at 0.7 (the recipe at AMT 70), and 0.9 ± 1e-6 at HEAD 0.6; HEAD 0.7 renders the same samples as HEAD 1, and HEAD 0.6 does not.
- **The numbers decision 3 rests on.** The re-created probe prints both tables; tests pin the jangle's 3.5 dB and the snare's −7.8 dB at the pad sheet's AMT 70 with the recipe's MIX 0.75, with stated margins.
- **The rack's contract tests pass unchanged**, with no exemption added (peak within 0.05 holds because ECHO's own peak match runs on any feed).
- **`delayed`** sets only `echo`, no macro on its neutral, a bypass at AMT 0, and `TreatmentsTest`'s names list ends with it.
- **RIG and DELAY on one board.** At AMT 1 on the snare, the board grows at most `FxChain.MAX_CHAIN_TAIL_SECONDS` past the original: RIG's slapback lengthens the AMP pass and the POST pass caps only against its own input, so the board's own cap does the work here (a two-pick form of the three-tail-pick budget test the library's "As built" deferred while RIG was its only tail-making entry).

## Registration and hazards

- **No new section.** `FxChain` and its `SECTIONS` table do not change; `FxChain.VERSION` stays 1; `echo` keeps its saved key. HEAD is appended to `Echo.MACROS`, and only a recipe that sets HEAD writes the key (pinned above).
- **A kit saved with HEAD cannot be read by an older build.** `FxChain`'s constructor refuses a macro its section does not know, so an older app throws on `echo.HEAD`. ENSEMBLE's SECTION has the same edge; the app updates in one direction on one phone, and MPC exports carry audio, not recipes. Disclosed, not fixed.
- **No seeded draw moves.** `Echo.scramble` has no caller in main source (grepped on `c5274e0f`); BREED crosses only the macro keys its parents already hold (`Breed.kt` `crossMacros`); the remix bank indexes `Shuffle.TREATMENTS` with a seeded `Random`, and its `slapback` sets no HEAD.
- **Pinned lists.** `TreatmentsTest`'s names list gains `delayed`, and `PedalsTest`'s day-one test (its entries list, `listOf("DRIVE", "COMP", "WAH", "RIG")`, and its name) gains DELAY in the build: the one registry pin the ungated entry breaks (a scratch run of the library's tests with a stand-in `delayed` failed only that). `PedalsTest`'s laws and `PedalBoardTest`'s one-pick equivalence (every non-board entry at AMT 0.7 and 1 renders as its chip) then cover it unchanged. When `gated` flips after the gate, `PedalsTest`'s gated list gains DELAY, and `PadSheetTest`'s row-seven list, its inventory set and its two 33s (with the inventory test's name) move to 34; the six-wide ceiling holds at three chips. The ECHO tests that exist (MIX 0 is a copy, repeats land where TIME says, REPEAT lengthens the trail within the tail bound, an echoed kick is still a kick) are untouched by design.
- **The word.** HEAD also names a tape echo's playback-head selector. The app and the CLI show no per-section dials (the CLI names characters such as `delayed`, never macros), so only a kit's saved recipe shows the word; it is kept because the owner asked for it by that name.
- **Known edge: the head is the pad's start, not its hit.** A pad whose hit is not at its first frame repeats its first 120 ms rather than its hit. Five ways reach it: a pad trimmed with silence before its hit, from the DELAY chip alone; the CLI's `treat`, plain or `--pedals`, which stacks on the current audio (`KitBuilder.treatPad`: "Treatments stack"; `--pedals` calls `pedalPad` with no restore first), so `swelled` then `delayed`; and the sheet, which stacks too when the bin holds no earlier take (`NOT_BINNED`, `PadSheetScreen.kt`), so a bank-B `reversed` twin then the DELAY chip; PASTE on the phone and the CLI's `recipe` (`RecipeReplay.apply` calls `characterPad` or `pedalPad` with no restore, so it stacks whatever the bin holds); and BREED, whose crossed chain can carry a `swelled` or `reversed` parent's section beside a `delayed` parent's echo, SWELL (before the rack) or reverse (before ECHO) running first. A pedal board will reach it too, once a FADE IN pedal sits on the PRE side. ## Why it is built this way

- **A knob on ECHO, not a new pedal section.** The head-fed clip you liked is ECHO with a different input, and its repeats were also about 8 to 10 dB louder. The input becomes the HEAD knob, so the code keeps one delay loop and everything that already uses ECHO (the SLAP chip, SIREN's landing, saved kits) is untouched because HEAD starts at zero. The loudness comes from ECHO's own MIX instead: your 0.75 makes the repeats 3.5 dB louder than the plain delay's, still about 5 to 6 dB quieter than the clip you liked.
- **HEAD decides what repeats; MIX decides how loud.** On the choir, HEAD turns overlapping repeats into separate ones: at 100, between repeats the level drops from about the repeat's own level to 10 dB under it; at the first tap, 70, the plain repeats already dip about 5 dB and HEAD deepens that to about 10. On the guitars HEAD does little, under 2 dB, because the string keeps ringing under the repeats; the guitar's pick is only 2 dB above the string, so on the guitars it is MIX, not HEAD, that lifts the echo. The gate plays HEAD off at the same level so you can hear what HEAD alone does.
- **The first tap.** At 70 the jangle's echo stands 3.5 dB over the note (the clip you liked: 5.3; the chug's, 2.5) and the repeats are 151 ms apart, the spacing you kept. On the snare the repeat is 3.5 dB louder than the plain delay you passed at the same setting, about one and a half times its level.
- **The decision with a cost** is your MIX 0.75. It lifts the guitar's echo part of the way to the clip you liked (on the jangle at 70, 3.5 dB over the note, against 2.4 at MIX 0.5 and 5.3 in the clip you liked), and makes a drum's echo louder than the plain delay you passed. The gate plays 1.0 and 0.5 beside it, so you can move it by ear.
- **What HEAD trades away**, found when the build was reviewed and put on the gate so you can judge it. On a held note, HEAD on leaves almost no echo after the note ends, because the repeats only ever carried the note's start; HEAD off keeps that echo. And HEAD repeats a pad's first 120 ms, so a pad that starts quiet, like a reversed snare, gets faint repeats. A HEAD between off and full feeds some of the note's body too, which is the lever if you want a little of both.
