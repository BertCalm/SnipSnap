## 1. Summary

The characteristic gesture is **rolling contact → shot blast → coverage builds and the plate
stiffens → a hardened ring that keeps its tuning (PLATE) or gives it back (PANE)**.

A free metal or glass plate is rolled against and then blasted with shot. Each shot dents
the surface; the dented fraction (coverage) climbs toward saturation, and the plate's upper
partials climb with it while its ring tightens. When the blast stops, the plate rings out in
the state the blast left it in. What is computed: the roller's spin-down (Coulomb), the shot
schedule (Poisson, seeded), coverage (the random-dent recursion the peening industry uses),
the modal plate (`Modes.Bank`). What is analogy: coverage moving the modal frequencies and
damping. Real cold work raises yield strength and barely changes stiffness, so a real peened
plate's frequencies hardly move. What is not simulated: plastic flow, residual stress fields,
curvature, shot rebound, stick-slip friction, air.

## 2. Goals and scope

Musical goals: evolving metallic textures, long cinematic impacts, and a held peening loop.
Every one-shot these voices make runs 1.65 to 4.42 s, so every pad files as LOOP; a short
PERC-class voice is possible only by shortening the blast, which removes most of the
hardening.

V1 includes: two voices (PLATE, PANE), a rolling texture read from a seeded roughness table,
the shot blast, the coverage law, the stiffening map, HOLD as a periodic loop, velocity as
shot speed. V1 excludes: an implicit stick-slip solver (`Modes.Friction` exists, but the
roller is a texture, not a bow); position-dependent hardening (coverage is one number for
the whole plate); curvature; mode coupling (`Bank.connect` is not used); hi-hats. Mono,
offline, dry, no samples.

### Why it earns a pad (sibling check)

Nearest siblings:
- **THAW**: contact work heats a bounded latent layer; water loads the upper plate modes
  downward while the root holds "a few cents" (`Thaw.kt:22`, `:396`); control tick 176
  samples (`Thaw.kt:38`). Its stored stress spends itself as fracture clicks.
- **CISTERN**: a seeded landing field excites the same surface it lands on; landings add mass
  and raise losses (`Cistern.kt:24-39`); always LOOP; HOLD is a settled periodic cycle.

The one thing neither can do: **get stiffer with use**. Both load their modes downward
(water, mass). SCOUR's partials rise and its ring tightens as a function of how much it has
been hit, and the tail tells you how it was treated (PLATE keeps the hardening, PANE gives
it back). MERCURY shares the `Modes.Bank` and the metal and glass bodies but has no history.

The honest risk on the roster: this is the eighth engine whose every render lands LOOP by
length, in the struck-modal-with-a-mechanism mould the inventory calls heavy. It earns its
pad only if the hardening is audible. That is the R1 gate (section 16), and section 18 names
the lighter landing if it fails. The first harness listen heard it (section 20.1).

## 3. SnipSnap contract

- Offline mono 44.1 kHz (`Dsp.RATE`); synthesis at `Dsp.RATE * Dsp.OVERSAMPLE` (176.4 kHz).
- Output stage, house order, written out in `Scour.kt` (copy the sequence; `Gyre.finish` and
  `Mercury.finish` carry their own engines' choices): `Tide.bandLimit`
  (`Tide.kt:537`) → `Dsp.decimate` (`Dsp.kt:770`) → subtract mean, then the 20 Hz `OnePole`
  high-pass → `Dsp.levelTo(out, Dsp.RATE, Dsp.MELODIC_LOUDNESS_TARGET)` → `Dsp.fadeTail`.
- Every draw from streams seeded by `Dsp.seedFor("SCOUR", MODEL_VERSION, voice.name, <stream>)`
  (the CISTERN pattern, `Cistern.kt:359`), never from macros (section 6.2).
- Macros: TUNE RPM SHOT HARDEN TRAVERSE plus HOLD (five plus HOLD, inside the house's 3 to 6).
- `drumClassFor` returns LOOP whenever the render exceeds 1.5 s (all renders in section 20),
  and agrees with the render length to the frame because both come from one closed-form
  `renderFrames(voice, macros, velocity)`.
- HOLD seam: `Keys.seamError < Keys.MAX_SEAM_ERROR` (1e-3, `Keys.kt:147`) measured against a
  real preceding cycle.
- Registration: the checklist in section 14.

## 4. Signal flow

```text
voice, macros, velocity
        │
        ▼
[ScourMechanics @ ~1 kHz control tick: pure function of (voice, RPM, SHOT, TRAVERSE, HOLD, velocity, MODEL_VERSION)]
   roller v(t) = max(0, v0 − a·t)          shot times t_i = t_open + Σ gaps_i / λ     coverage S: per shot S += κ·e_i·(1 − S),
   contact phase φ(t) = ∫ revs·v dt        per-shot amp, landing x_i, energy e_i       between shots S *= e^(−ρ·dt); smoothed 15 ms
        │                                            │                                              │
        ▼                                            ▼                                              │
[ScourExciter @176.4 kHz]                                                                           │
   roller force = roll·v^1.5·table(φ)  ──┐                                                          │
   shot force   = amp_i·halfSine(4 smp) ─┴─(per-mode force: participation at rim / at x_i)──▶[ScourPlate: Modes.Bank, 6..8 modes]
                                                                                                    ▲
                                                                  (retune every 176 samples: f_k, t60_k from smoothed S and HARDEN)
                                                                                                    │
                                                                                     pickup Σ w_k·velocity_k
                                                                                                    ▼
                                                                [Output: bandLimit → decimate → DC → levelTo → fadeTail]
```

One-way everywhere: the plate never feeds back into the roller, the shots or the stress.
HARDEN and TUNE do not enter ScourMechanics, so turning them reruns only the plate and the
output stage.

## 5. Architecture and separation

| Unit | Input | Output | Testable alone by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ScourMechanics` | voice, settled macros, velocity | `Trace`: roller speed per tick, shot times, amplitudes, positions, energies, raw and smoothed `S` per tick, close and stop times | stress bound, monotone SHOT, stop time `v0/a`, schedule stretch under SHOT |
| `ScourExciter` | trace, note | per-mode force arrays at 4× | shot pulse width, roller bandwidth rises with speed |
| `ScourPlate` | forces, smoothed `S`, HARDEN, note | raw 4× audio, plus the mode frequencies it actually used per tick | root never moves, upper modes rise with HARDEN·S, ceiling guard |
| `ScourHold` | voice, macros | one loop buffer and its measured seam | seam against a real preceding cycle |
| `finish` | raw | the `Snip` | the house probes |

The `Trace` follows PITCHWHEEL's ledger idea (`Pitchwheel.kt`'s `Event`/`Trace`/`Diagnostics`):
the HARDEN test reads mode frequencies from it instead of guessing them from a spectrum.

## 6. The model

### 6.1 Roller (the draft's Stir)

```text
v0     = lin(RPM, .15, 1)                  normalised start speed
v(t)   = max(0, v0 − a·t)                  Coulomb spin-down: constant deceleration a (voice), stops at t_stop = v0/a
φ(t)   = ∫ REVS·v dt                       contact phase in revolutions, REVS = 5 rev/s at v = 1 (shape)
F_roll = roll · v^1.5 · R(φ mod 1)         R: a seeded, pink-tilted roughness table of 8192 points per revolution
```

Literal: the spin-down law and reading a fixed surface at the contact phase (the catalog's
"rolling contact" exciter, read at contact phase, not body phase). Because the table is read
faster at higher speed, the texture's bandwidth rises with speed by construction; RPM is not
a filter knob. The table repeats once per revolution, so RPM is also audible as a slow
rhythm. The roller acts at the rim (fixed participation weights).

### 6.2 Blast (the draft's Peen)

```text
λ        = expMap(SHOT, 3, 300) shots/s        during the blast window [t_open, t_open + W·(1 + 2·HOLD)]
t_i      = t_open + (g_1 + … + g_i)/λ           g: unit-rate exponential gaps, seeded by voice only
amp_i    = A · √(λ_ref/λ) · (1 + .2·j_i) · vel   λ_ref = λ at SHOT .5; j_i seeded jitter
x_i      = lin(TRAVERSE, .05, .95) + .05·k_i     landing position, k_i seeded jitter
e_i      = (1 + .2·j_i) · vel^0.8               the shot's coverage weight (section 6.3)
pulse    = half-sine over 4 internal samples (22.7 µs)
```

The gaps are drawn once per voice and divided by λ, so turning SHOT stretches one fixed
schedule instead of reshuffling it. A knob turn changes density smoothly, and the same
patch always renders the same shots.

Literal: the Poisson schedule. Real contact times for millimetre steel shot are about a
microsecond (Hertz), shorter than one 4× sample, so a 4-sample half-sine is a band-limit
choice, not a mallet model. The pulse spectrum is flat across the audio band either way.

### 6.3 Coverage (the stress)

```text
per shot:        S ← S + κ · e_i · (1 − S)
per tick:        S ← S · exp(−ρ · CTRL/rate)
smoothed:        S̃ ← S̃ + (1 − e^(−CTRL/(rate·0.015))) · (S − S̃)
closed form:     S(t_close) ≈ S* · (1 − e^(−(λκ + ρ)·W)),   S* = λκ/(λκ + ρ)      (used for render length only)
```

Literal: with κ the fraction of the plate one dent covers, N random dents leave
`1 − (1 − κ)^N ≈ 1 − e^(−κN)` covered, which is the coverage relation the peening literature
uses (`C = 1 − exp(−A_r)`, Kirk; SAE J2277 calls 98 % "full coverage"). The recursion is that
relation one dent at a time. ρ, the recovery rate, is analogy (annealing is far slower than
this in real metal); it gives the two voices their different tails.

Bound, by construction: if `0 ≤ S < 1`, `0 ≤ κ·e ≤ 1` and the relaxation factor is in
(0, 1], then `S + κe(1 − S) = 1 − (1 − S)(1 − κe)` stays in [0, 1), and relaxation only
lowers it. No clamp. Measured worst case over the macro cube: 0.928 (PLATE), 0.878 (PANE).

Velocity enters as shot speed: Hertzian indentation depth scales as `v^(4/5)`, contact radius
as `√(R·δ)`, so dent area, and with it κ per shot, scales as `v^(4/5)`. Harder hits harden
faster, which is a change in event energy, not just level.

### 6.4 Stiffening (analogy) and the ring

```text
f_k(t)   = f0 · r_k · (1 + shift · HARDEN · S̃(t) · g_k),   g_k = k/(K − 1), k = 0 is the root: g_0 = 0
t60_k(t) = t60 · decay^k / (1 + damp · HARDEN · S̃(t))
```

The grade `g_k` makes the top mode move most and the root not at all: the partial set
stretches above a fixed note, the way a stiffer bar's partials stretch above its first. The
draft's `ω0·(1 + H·S)` for every mode above the first moved the second partial up by as
much as an octave at the corner. Here the second partial moves at most `shift/7` (5 % on PLATE)
and the top one at most `shift` (35 % on PLATE). The ring shortens as it hardens (the
draft's direction); whether a harder plate should ring longer instead is a listening
question for R1 (ledger).

After the blast closes, `S` keeps relaxing at ρ: PLATE (ρ = .15/s, time constant 6.7 s)
keeps its tuning through the tail, while PANE (ρ = 1.5/s, 0.67 s) slides back down while it
rings.

### 6.5 Stability

- The plate is `Modes.Bank` with no springs: every step is an exact rotation and a shrink
  (`Modes.kt:443`), and `tune` keeps `|z|`, so retuning cannot pump energy
  (`Modes.kt:445-448`). `MAX_NODE_KAPPA` does not bind because nothing is `connect`ed.
- Every path is one-way (section 4). There is no feedback loop to bound.
- Coverage is bounded by construction (6.3); the smoother is a stable one-pole.
- The roller force is bounded (`|R| ≤ 1`, `v ≤ 1`); the shot force is bounded per shot and
  the count is bounded by `λ_max·W_max`.

## 7. Excitation and energy budget

Two quantities, one source, kept apart on purpose:
- **What the plate hears** (audio drive): per-shot amplitude `∝ √(λ_ref/λ)`, so the expected
  mean shot power is the same at every SHOT. This is the house energy doctrine, set by one
  fixed law of λ, never by normalising events after the fact. Measured realised power
  against the default: −4.21 dB at SHOT .1 (about four shots in the window, so Poisson count
  dominates) and −0.53 dB at SHOT .9 (PLATE); −0.98 and +0.05 dB (PANE). The draft's probe
  "identical at SHOT 0.1 and 0.9" becomes two probes: the law test (`λ·amp² = const` exactly)
  and a realised test within ±1 dB when `λ·W ≥ 20` shots.
- **What the metal remembers** (mechanical drive): coverage counts dents, not audio energy.
  SHOT means "a flow of finer shot at constant blast power": more, quieter shots that cover
  more surface per second. That is why SHOT moves the stress (section 0, row 2).

The draft's "energy injected per millisecond is capped" is dropped: no cap is needed when the
law bounds the expected power and the count is bounded, and a per-millisecond cap would be a
limiter on the excitation.

## 8. Resonators and root

- `Modes.Bank(K, 176400)`, K = 8 (PLATE) or 6 (PANE), tuned before the first `step`, retuned
  every 176 samples. Force lands through `drive` (velocity kick); the pickup is
  `velocityAlong(w)` with `w_k = gain^k`.
- Ratios: the completely free circular plate, sourced from Leissa, *Vibration of Plates*
  (NASA SP-160, 1969), Table 2.5, ν = 0.33, which gives `λ² = ωa²√(ρh/D)` per mode
  (n nodal diameters, s nodal circles). Read from the OCR text of the NTRS scan:

  | mode (n, s) | (2,0) | (0,1) | (3,0) | (1,1) | (4,0) | (5,0) | (2,1) | (0,2) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | λ² | 5.253 | 9.084 | 12.23 | 20.52 | 21.6* | 33.1* | 35.25 | 38.55 |
  | ratio to (2,0) | 1 | 1.729 | 2.328 | 3.906 | 4.112 | 6.301 | 6.710 | 7.339 |

  \* Leissa marks these "true within 2 percent". The prototype's rounded table (1, 1.73,
  2.33, 3.91, 4.11, 6.30, 6.71, 7.34) sits within 0.1 % of every sourced ratio, so the
  section 20 numbers stand. R1 writes the sourced values with this citation, per `Modes.kt`'s
  rule ("do not adjust a value here without a source").
- What the source adds about the strike: a mode with n nodal diameters has zero amplitude
  at the centre for every n ≥ 1, so a real centre strike excites only (0,1) and (0,2) and
  **not the root**, which is (2,0). The prototype's fixed root weight (below) is therefore a
  deliberate departure from the physics, kept so the note survives TRAVERSE. R1 either builds
  real participation from the (n, s) labels and limits TRAVERSE's range so the root stays
  excited, or keeps the fixed root weight and says so in the KDoc.
- Root: mode 0 never moves (`g_0 = 0`) and always takes full strike weight (TRAVERSE cannot
  put the note on a node). That is necessary, not sufficient. Measured with the house
  `Pitch.detect` (section 20): at the house window (0.05 s, 0.25 s) 18 of 22 renders sit
  within 50 cents, but PLATE TRAVERSE 0 read 87.8 Hz for a 261.6 Hz note (f0/3). In the tail
  after the blast, only 10 of 22 sit within 50 cents, with f0/3 readings at RPM 0 and
  TRAVERSE 1. The wrong readings carried confidence 0.58 to 0.81, so the confidence floor
  (0.3) does not catch them. A plausible cause: the plate's own ratios sit near multiples of f0/3
  (1.73 ≈ 5/3, 2.33 ≈ 7/3, 4.11 ≈ 12/3), which an autocorrelation reads as a period three
  times too long. The sourced table confirms the grid is the free plate's physics, not a
  recall error. R1 either shortens and quiets modes 1 and 2 against the root, or moves to a
  table off that grid, and the root probe runs at both windows.
- Ceiling: a mode whose hardened frequency could exceed 18 kHz at this note (`f0·r_k·(1 +
  shift)`) is dropped for the whole note, so nothing switches mid-render. `Bank.tune` refuses
  only past 88.2 kHz, so the engine owns this guard. With this table the top mode at
  TUNE 1, HARDEN 1 is about 5.2 kHz and all 8 modes are kept; the guard matters for any voice
  with higher ratios.
- Raw level: `Modes.Bank`'s velocity pickup has no `1/sin θ` growth (a force kick sets
  velocity directly), but the radiation trim is a fixed constant measured once (the CISTERN
  habit), never a per-render normalisation before `levelTo`.

## 11. HOLD

Strategy: **periodic by construction** (CISTERN's settled cycle, `Cistern.kt:35-39`),
not Bore's cut-from-held. The draft's cut from a stochastic render cannot pass
`Keys.seamError`: what plays before the wrap is unrelated noise to what plays before the
loop start. Measured 0.255 to 4.55 against the 1e-3 bar.

```text
loop    = whole roller revolutions nearest 2 s, rounded to a multiple of CTRL (176 samples)
          (so the stress orbit is periodic to the tick); the roller speed moves by < 0.05 % to fit
blast   = one seeded shot schedule over [0, loop), repeated every cycle; roller at constant v0
stress  = iterate the control-rate coverage map over one cycle until it repeats to 1e-12
          (measured 4 to 28 passes of about 2,000 ticks), and use that orbit for every cycle
audio   = warm-up cycle(s) covering the plate's memory, then one loop; seam measured with the
          warm-up's last 256 frames as the real preceding cycle
```

Measured: seam 3.86e-9 (PLATE default), 1.42e-12 (PLATE SHOT 1 HARDEN 1), 1.67e-10 (PANE
default), 1.41e-13 (PANE corner), one warm-up cycle, loop 1.976 s at the default RPM. Held
stress 0.72, 0.96, 0.41, 0.86.

Disclosed: the loop is a driven adaptation, not the one-shot continuing. It holds the roller
at constant speed and the plate at a peened steady state where coverage balances recovery.
The same shots repeat every loop (about 60 at the default SHOT); a 4 s loop halves the
repetition rate at twice the render cost. The host stores one loop buffer, so the one-shot's
roller lead and first hardening are absent from HOLD exports (the THAW disclosure).
Rotate the exported loop to a quiet zero crossing with `Siren.bestCut` before levelling.

## 13. Numerical and output requirements

- Determinism: byte-identical renders; streams seeded per section 3. Measured in the
  harness: two renders equal.
- Finite: every macro corner and velocity extreme; a non-finite macro falls back to its
  default (`settled`).
- Peak: output 0.01..1 after `levelTo`.
- DC: |mean| under 0.005 after the house stage (measured 0 to 1e-6). No source of DC exists
  upstream (section 0).
- Aliasing: no partial above output Nyquist; the mode ceiling (section 8) is the guard.

  window, not the whole render, with a sign flip to prove it can fail (the SILK lesson).

## 17. Sonic risks (where this would sound bad)

1. **A weak root in the tail.** Measured: 10 of 22 tail windows read more than 100 cents
   off, some at f0/3. Caught by the two-window root probe.
2. **HARDEN too subtle.** Overall brightness falls during every render whatever HARDEN does,
   and HARDEN's activity measured 1.69 dB. Caught by the trace probe and the audition corners;
   the R1 gate. The first harness listen heard HARDEN 1 clearly (section 20.1), so this risk
   is now about the other corners and PANE, not PLATE at SHOT .7.
3. **The roller reads as hiss, not rolling.** Caught by the RPM sweep in the audition.
4. **TRAVERSE is dead or breaks the note.** Measured 0.87 dB and an f0/3 reading at 0.
   If R1 cannot fix it with the sourced mode shapes, make TRAVERSE the nozzle sweeping across
   the plate during the blast (a gesture, not a static weighting), or drop to four macros.
5. **The HOLD loop audibly repeats** (the same shots every loop). Caught by the LOOP ×8
   section.
6. **Retune clicks**, the draft's risk 2, is retired by construction: `Bank.tune` keeps
   `|z|`, and control-rate retuning of the smoothed stress measured −38.9 dB from
   per-sample retuning. The unsmoothed per-sample stress differs by 0.36 dB of log-spectral

## 18. Directions considered

Applying different operators to the same brief:

- **Strong, this spec (STACK, plus a literal PORT of coverage):** gesture clear
  (roll → blast → stiffen → hardened ring); novelty: the only modal engine that loads its
  modes upward; risk: a weak plate root and a subtle HARDEN, both measured above; cost: R1 to
  R4, the full set of integration surfaces. It wins because it keeps the draft's identity and
  macro story, every repair is measured, and it needs no toolkit change.
- **Alternate A, TUMBLER (MUTATE, literal PORT):** one RPM clock for a rotating drum of media
  passes through the drum regimes (rolling, cascading, cataracting, centrifuging; Mellmann
  2001, governed by the Froude number), so the friction and the shots come from one quantity
  and the gesture is a spin-up and spin-down, ending in the silence of centrifuging. More
  novel and one knob fewer; riskier, because the regime-to-audio mapping is shape and the
  spin trajectory is a schedule unless motor torque against load is modelled. Same cost.
- **Alternate B, the lightest landing:** a coverage helper beside `Modes.Bank` and a CISTERN
  voice whose landings stiffen instead of load. Keeps "hits harden it", drops the roller,
  and touches a handful of files instead of a new engine's full set; risk: it blurs
  CISTERN's finite-inventory identity. The fallback if the R1 gate fails.

## Why it is built this way

1. **The stress counts dents, not loudness.** The draft made each shot quieter as shots got
   denser (so the mix does not swell), then fed that same quieter energy into the stress.
   The two cancelled, and SHOT stopped changing how hard the metal got. Counting dents is
   what peening engineers actually measure, and it keeps "how loud the plate is hit" and
   "how much the metal changes" as two separate answers to two separate questions.
2. **The stress cannot pass 1 by its own arithmetic.** Each dent covers part of what is
   still uncovered, so the total approaches 1 and never arrives. That replaces a clamp, which
   would put a hard corner in the tuning trajectory exactly when the sound is most intense.
3. **The plate is retuned a thousand times a second, not every sample.** `Modes.Bank` can
   retune on any sample without a click, but doing it 176,400 times a second costs 14 times
   as much for a difference measured at −39 dB. THAW already runs at this tick.
4. **The loop is built to repeat; it is not cut from something that does not.** A random
   texture has no seam you can find afterwards, which is why the draft's cut measured up to
   4,550 times over the bar. Making one revolution's worth of shots repeat, and letting the
   stress settle into its own repeating cycle first, closes the seam to about a billionth.
5. **The risky decision is the plate itself.** A plate's pitch is weak by nature, and its
   ratios (now sourced from Leissa) sit on a grid that can make the note read a twelfth low
   in the tail. Fixing the root mode is necessary but not enough. The Kotlin audition decides
   whether this is a pitched instrument or a texture.

### What changed against the design

- **Modes 1 and 2 are quieter and shorter.** In Kotlin, at the design's levels, the house
  `Pitch.detect` misread the note in 59 of 162 PLATE corners at the onset window (often f0/3, as
  section 8 warned) and 56 of 162 in the tail; PANE 53 and 56. Quieting the whole gain law fixed the
  pitch but cost HARDEN a quarter of its reach (1.72 to 1.29 dB of log-spectral distance). Quieting
  only (0,1) and (3,0), the pair whose ratios line up every three root periods, to 0.4 of the gain law
  and 0.5 of their ring, fixed it without the cost: PLATE 162 of 162 at both windows, PANE 150 of 162
  at the onset and 162 of 162 in the tail, HARDEN 1.80 dB (PLATE) and 1.78 dB (PANE). The 0.4 and 0.5
  are shape. Reverting them alone fails `the requested note is the detected root...` at the first
  corner (PLATE, everything at 0: 102 cents flat).
- **The ratios are Leissa's own λ² values**, divided in the code, not the prototype's rounded table.
- **Velocity is part of the length.** A softer hit leaves less coverage and rings longer, so
  velocity 1 gives the shortest render. `drumClassFor` reads velocity 1, which keeps it right at
  every velocity whenever it says LOOP; a test checks the soft render is never shorter.
- **HOLD below its top lengthens the blast** (`W·(1 + 2·HOLD)`); the top step renders as the step
  below it, GYRE's pattern, until R2 builds the loop.
- **The mode ceiling ignores HARDEN**: a mode is dropped if its fully hardened frequency could pass
  18 kHz at the note, so HARDEN can never switch a mode off halfway through a sweep.
- **Draws**: three `kotlin.random.Random` streams seeded by `Dsp.seedFor("SCOUR", MODEL_VERSION,
  voice, GAPS / LEVEL / AIM)` (the CISTERN habit), and the roughness table from `Dsp.Noise` given its
  pink tilt by `Fft`. Turning SHOT keeps every shot's own draws (tested).
- **An audition-only ring switch**, `Scour.RingLaw.OPENS`, renders the same blast with hardening
  lengthening the ring instead of shortening it, so the owner's open question can be answered by ear.
  No macro, patch or recipe can reach it.
- **Raw level**: a fixed trim of 36. The first trim (60) let the loudest corner reach a raw peak of
  1.016 after the output stage (PLATE, SHOT 0, TRAVERSE 0: the energy law makes each of the few shots
  about three times heavier, and they pile onto the long ring), which the audition's raw export
  refused. At 36 that corner peaks at 0.61 and the defaults at 0.21 (PLATE) and 0.11 (PANE).

### What changed

- **RING replaces TRAVERSE.** TRAVERSE was the weakest control by measurement (1.13 dB, R1) and by ear.
  RING sets which way and how far hardening moves every mode's t60: `× f^(2(2·RING − 1))` with
  `f = 1 + damp·HARDEN·S`. RING .25 is R1's law, .5 leaves the ring alone, .75 (the default) is the
  "longer" clip the owner picked, and the ends double either way. A negative exponent is taken as a
  reciprocal so .25 is R1's `1 / f` exactly; before the (1,1) change below, RING .25 rendered R1's
  PLATE byte for byte (SHA-256 of the default and of SHOT .8 HARDEN 1, measured). The blast now lands
  where R1's TRAVERSE default put it, through R1's own float law, and `MODEL_VERSION` stays 1 so every
  shot keeps its draws.
- **A floor on the render length**, 1.6 s. RING 0 can tighten PANE to 1.33 s in all (RING 0, HARDEN 1,
  SHOT 1), under the classifier's 1.5 s, where its feature rules could file a plate as a drum. Every
  SCOUR render is still LOOP. Because the length now moves with velocity one way or the other
  depending on RING, `drumClassFor` reads velocity 0 and 1 and takes the shorter.

  series, but in full it reads badly: its overtones sit near half-integer ratios (1.444, 2.549, 4.347,

  `Pitch.detect` misread the note, mostly an octave low, in 132 of 243 grid corners at the onset. Listening on the

- **The square plate is solved here, not read from a table.** Leissa's ν = 0.225 tables (SP-160,

  entries of 4.62 (266.0 and 316.1, Ritz's own few-term upper bounds) sit below the converged values

  ν = 0.3 table (4.65: 19.5961 to 131.4697) to within 2.9e-5, and lands inside his ν = 0.225 bounds on

### Why R1.1 is built this way

1. **RING is one number with two named points on it.** .25 is exactly the "shorter" clip and .75 exactly
   the "longer" clip the owner picked, so the first listen's answer is a setting, not a guess, and
   turning the knob either way from it stays on the same law. It does nothing until the plate is
   hardened, the same way HARDEN does nothing without shots.
2. **PANE sounds different because it is a different shape.** A square plate rings with a different
   set of overtones than a round one. The catch is that a square's overtones nearly line up with the
   harmonics of a note an octave down, so heard everywhere it sounds like the wrong note.
3. **Where you listen is a physical choice, not a filter.** Along the diagonal, and 72 % of the way to
   the corner, three of the troublesome modes are perfectly still, so a pickup there simply does not
   hear them. Instrument makers do the same with where they strike a bar or a string.
4. **The quieter overtones are measured, not guessed.** PLATE's (1,1) mode at .6 and PANE's pair at .5
   are the gentlest settings at which every asserted corner still plays the note it was asked for;
   one step gentler, a corner (or a default) goes out of tune.
5. **The risky decision is still the plate's pitch.** Both voices are in tune where the tests look; at
   a full blast (PLATE) and a slow roller at C3 (PANE) the note can still wander. The phrases on the
   audition are where that either matters or does not.

  - **Shot rain.** Its rate rises twentyfold, exponentially, over the build and ends at SHOT's rate.
    Each unit-rate arrival `u` lands where the build's expected count reaches it,

    keeps its draws.

    reads one length and velocity moves only the hit.

### Why R2 is built this way

1. **The "static" became the build.** What sounded like noise sitting on top of a note in a riff is the
   classic material of a riser when it grows: faster, louder and brighter, then stops at a hit.
2. **The rain speeds up on a log scale.** Twentyfold over the build sounds like steady acceleration rather
   than a sudden rush at the end, and keeping each shot's own random draw means BUILD and SHOT reshape one

3. **The loudness ramp is a separate, named curve.** The energy law keeps "more shots" from simply meaning

### Disclosed, not fixed

- The rumble is quieter than the first sketch. The gain law that lets C2 read leaves the modes above 4.5
  times the root under 1 % of the hit's energy at C3. At the first listening point, a .78 gain law gave
  4 %. If SHEET reads as a low gong, the gain law is the lever, and C2's pitch read is what it costs.
- Bank B's "short hits" are short to the slam (0.25 s); each still rings 6.4 s.
- SHEET's root, 65 to 262 Hz over TUNE, has not been heard on a phone speaker or through an MPC, where
  the lowest octave may thin out.
- The R4 page compares the voices at the same TUNE, which puts SHEET an octave under the other two; it does
  not set SHEET against PLATE and PANE at the same pitch (SHEET at TUNE .5 against them at TUNE 0).
- `MODEL_VERSION` stays 1. SHEET is new, and PLATE's and PANE's renders are unchanged to the bit (their trim
  share is exactly 1).
- BEAM, the phone's synth picker and a docs page are still to come.

### Why R4 is built this way

1. **A thunder sheet is a plate, so it gets a plate solve.** The same Ritz solve that checks against Leissa
   gives the long sheet its own overtones, rather than a borrowed table.
2. **The note is fixed where it is heard, not with a filter.** Moving the listening point onto a mode's
   nodal line is what PANE did in R1.1. It removes the one partial that fought the root, and it leaves
   every other partial's level physical.
3. **An octave down is the reason for the voice.** PLATE and PANE already cover C3 to C5. SHEET's job is
   weight under a drop, so its lowest note is C2, and the voicing was measured at C2, where reading the
   pitch is hardest.
4. **Bank B, not a reshuffle.** The owner said yes to bank A as it is, so SHEET's pads go beside it in
   the same order: builds at two tempos, then presets, plus a low line of hits.

  - The root rings 3 s. Steel keeps its hardening (κ .015, ρ .12/s), with PLATE's stretch (.3).

  - BEAM plays from C3, like PLATE and PANE.
