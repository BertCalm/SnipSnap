GATECRASH

strike → the room rises to meet it → held flat → cut to black

Three 1980s drum kits for the FRESH TAPE shelf. Each pad's room is crushed until it stands as loud as the drum, then cut off on the drum's own timing. Tap a pad to hear it.

Measured, not heard. Every setting here was chosen without ears. The numbers prove the mechanism works. Only you can say whether it sounds right.

Laid out like the hardware: A01 is bottom left. The small trace on each pad is its real level over time.

Groove

Two bars at the kit's tempo, played the way the app plays a pad: velocity picks the soft, medium or hard zone, each hit takes the next of four takes, and a hat cuts the hat before it. The last half bar is a run on the single tom pads, so you can compare it with the fill on A15, where the toms share one room.

What to listen for

  1. Is the room too loud? On Stone Room's Wall Snare the held room peaks 2.6 dB above the dry hit. That is "turned to 11". If it is too much, it is one number per pad.
  2. Does the cut click? The room shuts in 10 to 25 ms. Listen on the Floor Tom (A12).
  3. Do soft strikes sound like the same drum? Use "Play all 12 strikes": four soft, four medium, four hard. Soft ones are duller and drier on purpose.
  4. Are the four takes different enough? Toms, kicks and snares in Stone Room are fresh strikes. Hats, claps and the machine drums only shift slightly.
  5. Machine Pop's toms: the pitch dive keeps ringing after the burst stops. Good, or should it be cut too?
  6. Are these three kits or one? They use different drums and two different ways of making the space.

How to tell me what you heard

The most useful notes name a kit, a pad and a direction. For example: "Stone Room A02: less room, longer hold" or "Electro Funk A01: shorter". "Sounds good, proceed" also works and gets recorded as approval of this baseline.

Everything is mono. The records this sound comes from are wide; this page is not.
