ARCO passes or fails on a three-second held note, bowed against a synth, not on a one-second stab. If you cannot pick the bowed note out from the synth, or the bowed note itself sounds like a synth, the engine fails.

That is the first card below. For each voice, at three pitches (the root, the middle and a high step), you get three clips of three seconds of bow each, unlabelled: A, B and C. For CELLO one is bowed (this engine), one plucked and one a sustaining synth; for ERHU one is bowed, one a sung voice and one a sustaining synth. Press the pick on the one you think is bowed, say how sure you are, then press REVEAL: it shows what each clip was and whether you were right, and locks your pick. Do all six before reading anything else.

WHAT YOU ARE LISTENING FOR

ARCO is a bowed string, not a struck one: a bow drags a string, the hair grips it and carries it along, the string's own tension pulls it back until the grip lets go, and that stick and slip, once every period, is the note. There is no sample and no oscillator under it, and no noise: the same knobs always make the same sound.

Nothing here has been heard by anyone yet. Every constant was measured (the string speaks once a period, is in tune, starts and stops where it should) and the measurements can say that, not that it sounds like a bow. That is this page's whole job. The constants marked “listening” in the source (the stroke's bite, the box, the vibrato) are guesses waiting for you.

After the held notes comes the one-second stab check: the cello stab at BOW 1 and BOW 0.5 beside a brass stab from each of two synths, in a two-bar pattern at 90 bpm with a snare on the backbeat, every stab at the same loudness; the pairs alternate on the bar line. Then HOLD THE SURFACE: press and hold a loop, slide PITCH while it sounds, let go. Then the kit as it lands on the MPC, then each voice's default, BOW, GRIP, BODY and HOLD at both ends, its eight presets, its LOOP and the vibrato with and without. Mark each YES (it sounds like the bowed string it says), NEARLY, or NO.

Known limits, so you do not have to find them: a low CELLO note takes a third of a second to build under the bow, so the lowest stab blooms; the LOOP step has no vibrato; ERHU spans 19 semitones, D4 to A5, not 24 (D6, the 24th semitone, has no bow pressure that slips once a period, and ERHU stops two semitones short of the last step that does); and the comparators are plain: the synth is set to rise as slowly as the bowed note does (the reveal says how long that is) but rises in a straight line and has no vibrato, the plucked CELLO comparator begins with a strike and rings out the way a plucked string does, and the sung ERHU comparator begins with a vowel, holds it for only about 1.8 s and has faded away by about 2.6 s, an octave low at the middle and high steps. The reveal says which is which.
