WHAT YOU ARE LISTENING FOR

BORE is a blown pipe, not a struck one: a valve (an air jet for FLUTE, a single reed for SAX) opens and shuts against a column of air whose length sets the pitch, and above a certain breath the note sustains itself for as long as the player blows. There is no sample and no oscillator underneath: the breath noise, the way the note starts, and the pitch pulling with the breath all come out of the same loop.

Not everything here has been heard. The SAX’s bite, rasp and voicing each went through a listening round, and its attack has had one first listen (below); the new attack cards after it have not been heard. Every constant was measured (speaking windows, pitch, onset, harmonics) and every preset was authored from those measurements, but the measurements can only say the loop speaks in tune, not that it sounds like a woodwind. That is this page's whole job. If the FLUTE reads as a clarinet, or the SAX as a buzzy synth lead, say so plainly: the constants marked “listening” in the source (breath noise, the chiff burst, the thump, the reed's bell) are guesses waiting for exactly that.

New in this round, and first below: the SAX’s loop. You marked the kit’s SOLO LOOP as sounding like a cheap keyboard. The first rounds had left the rasp and the voicing out of loops because they made loops fail to close, and put that down to a wrap that wanted a crossfade. That diagnosis was wrong: the loop’s pitch match was looser than it read (it said exact and was up to 0.2 of a frame out), and the voicing’s loudness-following is a gain that cannot repeat across a wrap. Both are fixed, so a loop now carries the rasp and the voicing and closes about sixteen times better than before (median seam 1.4e-6 against 2.2e-5; the bar is 1e-3). The first two cards are the SOLO LOOP and the PAD REED, each as BEFORE (the old loop), TWO-THIRDS (the rasp and 0.67 of the voicing, which is what a one-shot’s bite is at C3 with the default knobs and a little under it elsewhere) and SHIPPED (the whole of it), then the one-shot of the same note. Turn REPEAT on, and say whether it still sounds like a cheap keyboard, which of TWO-THIRDS and SHIPPED is right, and whether the wrap clicks. A whole-strength loop is brighter than its one-shot at low notes (13.8 dB of bite at C3 against 6.2), which is why TWO-THIRDS is there. A few extreme knob corners at low notes still never close, as before. It is measured, not heard.

Last round, now second below: the SAX’s attack. A real tenor reaches 80% of a note’s level in 0.05–0.12 s; ours took 0.44 s at C3 and 0.11 s at C5, because a reed that starts from a tiny seed grows by a fixed factor per period, so a low note is slow in seconds, and every note swelled where a saxophone attacks. The fix is a short tone at the note’s own pitch put into the pipe at the tongue’s release (the “seed”), scaled by CHIFF, BREATH and the reed’s own level: CHIFF 0 is still the slow swell and a soft breath stays slow. The third card is the same phrase at four seed strengths — NO SEED (the old swell), LIGHT, SHIPPED, STRONG — and each clip’s line says how long that very note took to speak. The brightness now also blooms after the level, so the faster start does not read as a snare to the classifier. First listen: the four seeds sounded identical. The files do differ (the pitched tone is at full level by 0.06 s in SHIPPED and about 0.3 s in NO SEED) but every clip begins with the same pop and breath burst, and the notes are short, so the first two cards are now long notes after a quiet lead-in, and the seed alone with no pop and no chiff. Say whether those two differ. Then, say whether the attack reads as a tongued reed note, whether SHIPPED is the right strength, and whether a hard tongue (CHIFF 1) is an accent or a blat. The first listen found no difference between the seeds, so what the two new cards sound like is not yet known.

Before that, and third below: the SAX’s voicing. The question then was what is still missing, so a real tenor sax (UNSW’s recordings, e.g. their D4 page, the same pitch as our C3) was measured against ours: in the real one the fundamental is rarely the strongest partial, most of the power sits at 250–1000 Hz, and the highs are far stronger than ours were. You kept the deeper cut of the fundamental region and the brighter lift, and cut the version without the rasp. This round builds both in: the fundamental region cut and everything over 3 kHz lifted, opening with the note’s loudness (a saxophone is brighter the louder it plays, and a voicing at full strength from the first millisecond made every preset read as a snare to the drum classifier). The first card is the same phrase at four strengths — MERGED (the round before), MILDER, SHIPPED, STRONGER — then three presets. The held LOOP did not carry it then; since the loop round it does. Say which strength is right and whether it reads as a saxophone. This round has been listened to.

First the kit as it lands on the MPC, then each voice's default, then BREATH, LIP, CHIFF and HOLD at both ends, then that voice's own eight presets, then the landing: the same note dry, with the TAPE recipe every one-shot pad lands with (WOBBLE .1), and with twice the wow. The tape is the rack's, not the engine's; the question is whether it earns its place in the recipe. Known limits, so you do not have to find them: SAX is quick to speak now but the high notes may be too quick (0.04 s at C5 against a real 0.05–0.12 s), and the top step of HOLD is a seamless two-second LOOP with no vibrato and no breath noise. Mark each KEEP, MEH or CUT. A CUT on a knob end means that end goes too far.
